Curriculum

Work through the parts in order, starting with Part 1. Each part has goals: when you finish it, you should be able to do the things listed. Lessons with a link are published; “Coming soon” lessons are being written.

19 of 19 lessons in Parts 1–6 are published.

Part 1. Contracts and pay

When you finish, you can
  • Check that your contract has the required content and is the right type.
  • Calculate your own overtime pay and compare it with your payslip.
  • Tell whether you are a leased worker, and whether your job may legally be leased.
  • Know how much time off you are entitled to, and work out your allowance when you leave.
  • Employment contracts in Vietnam: 8 things to know before you signWho signs, how many copies, probation limits, no ID retention or deposits, and when you can quit at once.
  • Wages, overtime and hazardous work: check your own payslip2026 regional minimum wages, what a payslip must show, overtime must be voluntary, how to calculate 150% – 200% – 300% – 270%, in-kind allowances.
  • Leased workers: working in a factory but employed by another companyLeasing is limited to 12 months and certain jobs, pay must equal direct employees doing the same work, and the leasing company must lodge a deposit and register with the Department of Home Affairs.
  • Working hours, weekly rest, public holidays and annual leave: how much time off you getNo more than 8 hours a day or 48 a week; 30–45 minute breaks; 24 hours' weekly rest; 11 public holidays plus 24 November; 12–16 days' annual leave and pay for leave not taken.
  • Leaving or losing your job: severance allowance and job-loss allowanceWho gets severance (half a month's wage per year), who gets the job-loss allowance (one month's wage per year, at least two months), why unemployment-insurance time is deducted, and how to work it out yourself.

Part 2. Insurance

When you finish, you can
  • Check your own social insurance record on the VssID app.
  • Know when you qualify for unemployment benefit and the filing deadline.
  • Know how to pay voluntary social insurance yourself when you no longer have a contract.
  • Use your health insurance card so the fund pays as much as possible.
  • Social and unemployment insurance: check that your employer really paysThe 2024 Social Insurance Law and 2025 Employment Law – pension after 15 years, unemployment insurance from 1-month contracts, what happens when employers evade contributions.
  • Voluntary social insurance: for people without an employment contractWho can join, how much to pay (at least VND 330,000 a month in 2026), 20–50% state support, a pension after 15 years, a VND 2 million maternity allowance, and when you can take a lump sum.
  • Health insurance: using your card at the clinicHow your employer pays your health insurance, what share the fund pays, where to go if you work far from home, what you get for self-referral from 01/07/2026, and what to bring.

Part 3. Safety and health at work

When you finish, you can
  • Spot hazards at your workplace and know what the employer must do.
  • Know the steps after a work accident and what you are entitled to.
  • Read the label and safety data sheet of the chemicals you use.
  • Know women workers' rights during pregnancy and while raising a young child.
  • Occupational safety and health: health checks, occupational diseases and what you can see yourselfWho gets a check every 6 months, gynaecological checks for women, 35 compensable occupational diseases, the right to refuse dangerous work, safety data sheets, emergency exits.
  • Work accidents and occupational diseases: what must the employer pay?Advance treatment costs, full wages while you recover, compensation by level of incapacity, extra benefits from the insurance fund, and accidents on the way to work.
  • Working with chemicals: reading labels and safety data sheetsWhat a chemical label shows, what the hazard pictograms mean, which sections of the safety data sheet (SDS) to read, and the training and protective equipment the employer must provide.
  • Women workers: pregnancy, young children and work to avoidNo night work or overtime from the 7th month of pregnancy; 30-minute breaks during menstruation and 60-minute breaks while raising a baby under 12 months; 6–7 months of maternity leave; no dismissal because of pregnancy.

Part 4. Standing up for your rights

When you finish, you can
  • Build your own evidence file when there is a dispute.
  • Know where to file a complaint and how to get free legal aid.
  • Recognise discrimination and sexual harassment, and know how to report them.
  • Keep evidence and complain the right wayA 7-item evidence kit, asking in writing, where to complain, labour mediators, time limits for disputes, and who gets free legal aid.
  • Discrimination and sexual harassment at workWhat counts as discrimination and as sexual harassment; where the “workplace” extends; what the employer must do; your right to quit without notice; fines from 10/09/2026.

Part 5. Working abroad

When you finish, you can
  • Recognise signs of fraud and forced labour before you leave.
  • Know who to call if you run into trouble abroad.
  • Prepare your documents before leaving; settle your contract and get your deposit back on return.
  • Working abroad: lawful fees, scam warning signs and forced labourFee caps under Law 69/2020; the ILO's 11 indicators of forced labour; Vietnamese-language help lines in the destination country.
  • Before you go abroad to work: contracts, costs and documents to keepThe file you need, what the contract must state, money nobody may take from you, the maximum deposit, the orientation course, and a checklist of documents to keep.
  • Coming home: closing your contract, getting your deposit back, insuranceClose the placement contract within 180 days, get your deposit back with interest, get service fees refunded if you return early through no fault of your own, social insurance while abroad, and help from the Overseas Employment Support Fund.

Part 6. Life skills

When you finish, you can
  • Make a monthly spending plan from your wages.
  • Recognise the traps of app loans and loan sharks.
  • Managing your monthly payRead your payslip, know what the employer may and may not deduct, make a spending plan, build an emergency fund, take care with rotating savings groups (hụi).
  • App loans and loan sharks: how to spot and avoid themThe maximum legal interest rate, signs of a “black” loan app, debt collection by threats and calls to your relatives is against the law, what to do if you have already borrowed.

How to study each lesson

  1. Read In short first.
  2. Read What the law says and compare it with your own papers (contract, payslip, social insurance record).
  3. Follow What you can do, step by step.
  4. Save the numbers in Who to call on your phone.
  5. Check the Checked on date: the law may have changed.
