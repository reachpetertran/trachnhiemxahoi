Social and unemployment insurance: check that your employer really pays

Checked on 18/11/2026 · Compiled by Liêm

In short
  • Since 01/07/2025, 15 years of contributions (instead of 20) can qualify you for a pension.
  • Since 01/01/2026, contracts of 1 month or more must include unemployment insurance.
  • Check your contribution record every month; if something is missing, ask in writing straight away.

Quotations of Vietnamese law below are unofficial translations. The Vietnamese text prevails.

1. Three kinds of insurance at work

  • Social insurance: sickness, maternity, work accidents, occupational diseases, retirement, survivors.
  • Health insurance: medical care.
  • Unemployment insurance: benefit when you lose your job, help with training and job search.

The employer pays its own share and deducts your share from your wages to pay on your behalf. If your payslip shows an insurance deduction that the social insurance agency has not recorded, it is a sign that the employer took your money but did not pay it in.

2. What is new in the 2024 Social Insurance Law (from 01/07/2025)

  • Pension: the minimum contribution period drops from 20 years to 15 years.
  • People who reach retirement age with less than 15 years of contributions, and are not yet old enough for the social pension, can receive a monthly allowance from their own contributions, with health insurance paid by the state.
  • The law has specific rules on late and evaded contributions (Articles 38–41). The employer must pay the full amount, plus 0.03% per day on the amount and days late or evaded. Evasion can also lead to criminal liability (Article 41).

Pension rate (as explained by Vinh Phuc provincial social insurance): with 15 years of contributions, men receive 40% and women 45% of average insured wages; the longer you contribute, the higher the rate.

3. Unemployment insurance under the 2025 Employment Law (from 01/01/2026)

  • Contracts of 1 month or more must now include unemployment insurance (previously 3 months or more).
  • Monthly benefit: 60% of the average insured wage of the last 6 months, capped at 5 times the regional minimum wage (Article 39(1)).
  • Start date: “the 11th working day from the date of submitting a complete application” (Article 39(3)). Some websites still say “the 16th day” – that was the old 2013 rule.
  • Conditions (Article 38): at least 12 months of contributions in the 24 months before leaving (for contracts of 1 to under 12 months: 12 months within 36 months); you did not quit unlawfully; and you submit a complete application within 3 months of the contract ending.

4. Workers outside compulsory insurance

Some workers (for example, some older workers) are not covered by compulsory social, health and unemployment insurance. In that case the employer may not keep that money:

Labour Code 2019, Article 168(3): the employer must “pay the employee, together with the wage, an additional amount equal to what the employer would contribute to compulsory social insurance, health insurance and unemployment insurance for the employee”.

5. What you can do

  1. Install Vietnam Social Security's VssID app (or ask the social insurance office where your employer pays) to see your contribution record.
  2. Take a screenshot every month and keep it with your payslip.
  3. Compare: note any month where your payslip shows an insurance deduction but the record does not, or where the insured wage is lower than your contract wage.
  4. Ask HR in writing and keep both the question and the answer.
  5. Not resolved: send a complaint with evidence to the social insurance office where your employer pays, or to the provincial Department of Home Affairs. See Keep evidence and complain the right way.
  6. Lost your job: apply for unemployment benefit within 3 months of leaving – miss the deadline and you lose that claim. Apply at the public employment service centre.

Informal workers and farmers can join voluntary social insurance. A separate article for them is being written.

Coming changes

Bills amending the Labour Code and the Employment Law are before the National Assembly (November 2026). Not law yet.

Sources
