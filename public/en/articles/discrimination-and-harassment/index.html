Discrimination and sexual harassment at work

Checked on 23/11/2026 · Compiled by Peter Tran

In short
  • Discrimination and sexual harassment at work are both prohibited.
  • Harassment can be physical, spoken, by message or by images – in the workshop, the dormitory, the company bus, at a party or in a work chat group.
  • If you are sexually harassed at work, you may end your contract without notice. The harasser can be dismissed and fined VND 15–30 million.

Quotations of Vietnamese law below are unofficial translations. The Vietnamese text prevails.

1. What the law says

Labour Code 2019, Article 3(8): “Discrimination in labour means distinguishing, excluding or giving preference on the basis of race, colour, national or social origin, ethnicity, sex, age, maternity status, marital status, religion, belief, political opinion, disability, family responsibilities, HIV status, or on the grounds of establishing, joining and taking part in a trade union or a workers' organisation at the enterprise, with the effect of affecting equality of opportunity in employment or occupation.”

It is not discrimination where the difference comes from the specific requirements of the job, or protects the jobs of vulnerable workers (same clause).

Article 3(9): “Sexual harassment at the workplace means any sexual act by any person towards another person at the workplace that the other person does not want or accept.”

Article 8 of the Labour Code prohibits both (clauses 1 and 3).

What sexual harassment includes – Decree 145/2020/NĐ-CP, Article 84(2):

  • Physical: “acts, gestures, contact or touching of the body of a sexual nature or with sexual suggestion”.
  • Verbal: words in person, by phone or electronically with sexual content or innuendo.
  • Non-verbal: body language; displaying or describing sexual images or material, including electronically.

It may be a trade – demanding sex in exchange for a job, a raise or an easy shift – or behaviour that makes the workplace “unpleasant and insecure” (clause 1).

The “workplace” is wider than you think (Article 84(3)): besides the workshop it includes seminars, training, official business trips, meals, phone calls, electronic communication, transport arranged by the employer and accommodation provided by the employer.

2. What the employer must do

  • Internal labour rules must cover prevention of sexual harassment and how it is handled (Labour Code, Article 118(2)(d)).
  • The rules must state: a ban on harassment; the specific acts; time limits and procedures for complaints and handling; disciplinary measures; compensation for the victim (Decree 145/2020, Article 85(1)).
  • Handling must be prompt and protect the confidentiality, honour and safety of the victim and the person reporting (Article 85(2)).
  • When there is a complaint, the employer “must promptly stop and deal with it” (Article 86(1)(c)).
  • A worker who sexually harasses others (as defined in the internal rules) can be dismissed (Labour Code, Article 125(2)).

3. Your rights

Labour Code 2019, Article 35(2): the employee may unilaterally terminate the contract without notice if “d) sexually harassed at the workplace”.

The same clause allows quitting without notice if you are mistreated, beaten, humiliated or subjected to forced labour (point c).

Working abroad under contract and sexually harassed: you may also end the contract unilaterally (Law 69/2020/QH14, Article 6(1)(đ)) – see Coming home.

Fines (Decree 283/2026/NĐ-CP, effective 10/09/2026):

Act Fine
Sexual harassment at work, not serious enough for criminal prosecution VND 15–30 million (Article 17(3)), plus a public apology, unless the victim asks in writing for none (clause 5(d))
Discrimination in labour VND 5–10 million (Article 11(2)(a))
Discrimination in employment (recruitment…) VND 5–10 million (Article 10(1))

These are amounts for individuals; a company (organisation) pays twice as much (Article 7(1)).

4. Common problems in factories

  • Rules say “harassment is prohibited” but there is no separate channel to report; victims must report to the very team leader who harasses them.
  • Touching, teasing or sharing sexual images in chat groups treated as “jokes”.
  • Moving or cutting the shifts of the person who complained instead of the accused.
  • Job ads saying “women under 25 only” or “no children”, without any specific job requirement.
  • Questions about marriage or children at interviews.
  • Different pay or bonuses for men and women doing the same work.

5. What you can do

  1. Safety first: avoid being alone with the harasser; ask a trusted colleague to come with you.
  2. Write it down at once: date, time, place, words, acts, witnesses. Screenshot messages and images; do not delete them.
  3. Report in writing through the channel in the internal rules (HR, internal hotline). Keep a copy and note the date. The workers' representative organisation at the enterprise (if any) must provide information, advice and representation to the victim (Decree 145/2020, Article 86(3)(b)).
  4. If the employer does nothing: complain to the Department of Home Affairs where you work; get free legal aid – see Keep evidence and complain.
  5. If you are groped, attacked or threatened: report to the police (113).
  6. If you want to leave at once: you may quit without notice (Article 35(2)(d)). Give written notice stating the reason and keep evidence.

6. Who to call

  • Immediate danger: 113.
  • If the person harassed is under 18: 111 (national child protection hotline).
  • Complaints and legal aid: see Support contacts.
Coming changes

A bill amending the Labour Code is before the 16th National Assembly. Not law yet. The draft amendment to Decree 145/2020/NĐ-CP (Ministry of Home Affairs, July 2026) has not been issued either.
