Before you go abroad to work: contracts, costs and documents to keep

Checked on 23/11/2026 · Compiled by Peter Tran

In short
  • Go only through a licensed company. The placement contract must state the service fee and every other cost.
  • Nobody may charge you a brokerage fee or a “selection fee”. A deposit must go into a frozen bank account in your own name.
  • Keep copies of every contract, receipt, passport and visa, and leave one set with your family.

Quotations of Vietnamese law below are unofficial translations. The Vietnamese text prevails.

1. What the law says

Conditions (Law 69/2020, Article 44): full legal capacity; going voluntarily; fit health; language and skill requirements of the foreign side; a certificate of completion of the orientation course; no exit ban.

Your file (Article 45): application to work abroad; certified CV; health certificate; orientation course certificate; diplomas and certificates required by the foreign side.

The placement contract is signed between you and the service company:

Law 69/2020, Article 21(2): the contract “must clearly set out the rights and obligations of each party, match the labour supply contract, and clearly state the agreed service fee and other costs of the worker (if any).”

Prohibited acts – Law 69/2020, Article 7 (extract):

“7. Using worker preparation or selection to collect money from workers unlawfully. 8. Collecting brokerage fees from workers. 9. Collecting service fees from workers contrary to this Law. 10. Applying any security measure other than a deposit or a guarantee provided for in this Law.”

So, to make sure you keep to the contract, the company may only use a deposit or a guarantee – it may not keep your original documents, make you sign an “IOU” or pay some other kind of “bond”. A guarantee is used only when you cannot afford the deposit (Article 56(1)) and requires a written guarantee contract (Article 58). The guarantor may agree to secure it with property (Article 59) – your relatives should read it carefully before signing.

The law also bans sending workers to certain jobs, for example massage in restaurants, hotels or entertainment centres; regular contact with explosives or radiation; shrouding and burying the dead (Article 7(12)); and to areas at war, contaminated by radiation or toxins, or with especially dangerous epidemics (clause 13).

2. What you pay: right and wrong

Item What the law says
Service fee Capped and collected only after the contract is signed – see the table in Working abroad (Article 23)
Brokerage fee Banned from workers (Article 7(8))
Selection fee A company collecting a selection fee is fined VND 50–70 million per worker (Decree 283/2026, Article 49(6))
Deposit By agreement, capped; paid into a frozen account in your name at a bank (Article 25(2))
Skills and language training Agreed separately with the company (Article 63). Stricter rules from 01/03/2027 – see “Coming changes”

Deposit – Decree 112/2021/NĐ-CP:

  • The deposit is made only after the placement contract is signed and the foreign side has accepted you or you have a visa (Article 30(2)).
  • Caps (Appendix II): Taiwan VND 12,000,000; Korea VND 36,000,000; Japan, the Americas, Southeast Asia, the Middle East: no deposit; deep-sea fishing and transport ship crews: no deposit; other countries: up to one economy air ticket home to Vietnam. Being verified (Decree 372/2025 amended Decree 112 – we are checking whether this appendix changed.)
  • Agreeing a deposit above the cap, or taking a deposit and not paying it into the frozen account, is fined (Decree 283/2026, Article 49(2) and (6)).

3. The orientation course: what you learn

Compulsory before departure (Law 69/2020, Article 6(2)(c); Article 65). It covers, quoting Article 65(1):

  • Vietnamese law and the law of the host country; the main contracts;
  • “skills in spending, saving and sending money home”;
  • “basic knowledge of forced labour, preventing human trafficking, gender equality, sexual abuse, gender-based violence and prevention skills”;
  • “addresses and hotlines supporting workers in difficulty”.

A company that does not run the course or issue the certificate is fined VND 10–20 million per worker (Decree 283/2026, Article 49(2)(a)).

Waiting time before departure: the company must commit in writing to a waiting time of no more than 180 days from when you pass selection. Failing to commit, or to keep the commitment, is fined VND 50–70 million (Decree 283/2026, Article 49(5)(g)).

4. Common problems

  • Paying “agents”, acquaintances or “officials” – not a licensed company – with no receipt.
  • Contracts only in a foreign language, not stating the service fee, or no copy given to the worker.
  • Workers made to sign an “IOU” or hand over original documents to “hold a place” – on top of the deposit or guarantee.
  • Deposits handed in cash to a company employee.
  • Discovering after the training that the job order does not exist; waiting far too long to depart.

5. What you can do

  1. Check the company's licence with the Department of Home Affairs or the Department of Overseas Labour (Ministry of Home Affairs) before paying anything.
  2. Read the contract before signing. It must state: country, workplace, job, duration, pay, hours, housing, service fee, other costs, who pays the air ticket. Ask about anything you do not understand.
  3. Every payment needs a receipt in the company's name; transfer to the company's account, not a personal one.
  4. Deposit: ask for a three-party deposit contract (you – the company – the bank) and a frozen account in your name.
  5. Keep a set of documents (photocopies, photos on your phone, and one set with your family): - placement contract; employment contract with the foreign employer; - receipts for the service fee and other costs; deposit contract and frozen-account book; - passport, visa, residence card; - orientation certificate; health certificate; - name, address and phone of the company, the employer and support hotlines in the host country.
  6. Save support numbers for the host country – see Working abroad.

6. Who to call

  • Suspected fraud or trafficking: call 111, or the police 113.
  • A company charging wrongly or refusing refunds: the Department of Home Affairs where you live; the Department of Overseas Labour; free legal aid – see Support contacts.
Coming changes

Law No. 22/2026/QH16 amends Law 69/2020, effective 01/03/2027 (not yet in force): - Companies must sign a written agreement with you when you join their worker-preparation programme, stating training, language and other fees. Training fees may be collected only after you are selected by the foreign employer and have signed the placement contract. - A company that fails to send you abroad within the committed time must compensate you and refund the service fee and other payments (minus costs already spent).
