Wages, overtime and hazardous work: check your own payslip

Checked on 18/11/2026 · Compiled by Liêm

In short
  • From 01/01/2026, the monthly minimum wage is VND 5,310,000 (Region I) down to VND 3,700,000 (Region IV).
  • Overtime needs your consent; maximum 40 hours a month and 200 hours a year (up to 300 hours in some sectors).
  • With every pay, the employer must give a pay statement showing wages, overtime, night pay and deductions.

Quotations of Vietnamese law below are unofficial translations. The Vietnamese text prevails.

1. Regional minimum wages from 01/01/2026

Under Decree 293/2025/NĐ-CP:

Region Per month Per hour
I VND 5,310,000 VND 25,500
II VND 4,730,000 VND 22,700
III VND 4,140,000 VND 20,000
IV VND 3,700,000 VND 17,800

The region depends on where the company (or branch) operates, not where you come from. The list of communes and wards in each region is in the decree's appendix. After the 2025 merger of provinces and communes, look up the new commune or ward name.

This is the lowest pay for the simplest work in normal conditions, for normal hours – overtime not included.

2. What your payslip must show

Article 95(3): “Each time wages are paid, the employer shall provide the employee with a pay statement clearly stating the wage, overtime pay, night work pay, and the items and amounts deducted (if any).”

A payslip that shows only the “total received” is not enough.

Pay on time: monthly wages are paid once a month or twice a month, on a fixed date. Even in force majeure, payment must not be more than 30 days late; if it is 15 days late or more, the employer must add at least the 1-month deposit interest (Article 97).

3. Overtime: voluntary and limited

Article 107(2): The employer may use overtime only when all conditions are met, including: “a) The employee agrees.”

Overtime limits (Article 107(2), (3), (4)):

  • no more than 50% of normal daily hours (with a weekly schedule: normal plus overtime hours no more than 12 a day);
  • no more than 40 hours a month;
  • no more than 200 hours a year; some sectors (textiles, garments, leather, footwear, electrical, electronics, processing of agricultural, forestry, salt and aquatic products…) may go up to 300 hours a year, and the company must notify in writing the provincial labour authority.

All three limits apply at the same time.

4. How overtime pay is calculated

Under Article 98, overtime pay is at least:

Overtime on At least
A normal working day 150%
The weekly rest day 200%
Public holidays, Tet, paid days off 300% (plus the holiday pay itself)
  • Night work (10 pm – 6 am): at least 30% extra.
  • Overtime at night: an extra 20% of the daytime pay for the same type of day.

Example (calculation guided by Decree 145/2020): the company's weekly rest day is Saturday, and you work the Saturday night shift:

200% (weekly rest day) + 30% (night work) + 20% × 200% (night overtime) = 270%.

In real audits we have seen companies pay only 130% for this shift – workers lost more than half of what they were owed.

Weekly rest: at least 24 consecutive hours off every week. The weekly rest day must be stated in the internal labour rules (Article 111). Besides the mid-shift break, the company must set short breaks in its internal rules (Article 109).

5. Heavy, hazardous and dangerous jobs

Is your job on the list? The list is set by Circular 11/2020/TT-BLĐTBXH, supplemented by Circular 19/2023/TT-BLĐTBXH. Time worked in jobs on older lists before 01/03/2021 still counts. If your job is listed, your rights differ:

  • More annual leave: 14 working days (heavy, hazardous, dangerous) or 16 working days (especially heavy, hazardous, dangerous), compared with 12 days for normal work – Article 113(1).
  • Health checks at least every 6 months – see Occupational safety and health.
  • In-kind allowance (food, drinks) per working day, when you do a listed job and the work environment has harmful factors above permitted limits. Circular 24/2022/TT-BLĐTBXH sets 4 levels: VND 13,000, 20,000, 26,000 and 32,000. Working 50% or more of normal daily hours earns the full ration; less than 50% earns half; overtime increases the ration in proportion (Article 4). The environmental condition refers to Circular 29/2021/TT-BLĐTBXH, which is no longer in force. Replacement being verified

Common problems: the company never identifies which jobs are hazardous; allowances below the set level; no allowance for overtime hours.

6. What you can do

  1. Record your own hours every day (start, finish, overtime) in a notebook or on your phone. Photograph the time sheet if you can see it.
  2. Compare your payslip with your hours using the table in section 4. If something is wrong, ask HR in writing (message or letter) and keep a copy.
  3. Don't want overtime: you may refuse. Say so clearly and keep messages if you are pressured.
  4. Unpaid or late wages: you may quit at once without notice – see Employment contracts, section 5.
  5. To complain: see Keep evidence and complain the right way.
Coming changes
  • Regional minimum wages are usually adjusted by a new decree issued late in the year. This article will be updated when a new decree is issued.
  • The draft Labour Code amendment proposes 2 more public holidays – under review, not law yet.
