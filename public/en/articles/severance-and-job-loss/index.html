Leaving or losing your job: severance allowance and job-loss allowance

Checked on 23/11/2026 · Compiled by Peter Tran

In short
  • If you have worked regularly for 12 months or more and your contract ends lawfully, you get a severance allowance of half a month's wage per year worked – minus the time you were covered by unemployment insurance.
  • If you are let go because of restructuring, new technology, economic reasons, a merger and so on, you get a job-loss allowance of one month's wage per year, and at least two months' wages.
  • The employer must pay within 14 working days of the contract ending (up to 30 days in some cases).

Quotations of Vietnamese law below are unofficial translations. The Vietnamese text prevails.

1. Two different allowances

Severance allowance Job-loss allowance
When Contract ends lawfully (expiry, mutual agreement, you resign lawfully, employer terminates lawfully…) Employer lets you go for restructuring, new technology, economic reasons, division, merger, sale of the business…
Amount Half a month's wage per year worked One month's wage per year worked, at least two months' wages
Article 46 47

Common condition: you have worked regularly for the employer for a full 12 months or more.

2. Severance allowance – what the law says

Labour Code 2019, Article 46(1): “When an employment contract ends under clauses 1, 2, 3, 4, 6, 7, 9 and 10 of Article 34 of this Code, the employer shall pay a severance allowance to employees who have worked regularly for it for a full 12 months or more, at half a month's wage for each year worked, except for those eligible for a pension under the social insurance law and the case in Article 36(1)(e) of this Code.”

Cases that do qualify (under Article 34) include:

  • The contract expires (clause 1); the work under the contract is finished (clause 2).
  • Both sides agree to end it (clause 3).
  • You resign lawfully – with the required notice, or in a case where you may leave immediately under Article 35 (clause 9).
  • The employer terminates lawfully under Article 36 (clause 10).
  • Some other cases: you receive a prison sentence without suspension (clause 4); you die or go missing (clause 6); the employer ceases operations (clause 7).

Cases that do not qualify:

  • Dismissal as a disciplinary measure.
  • Abandoning your job without good reason for 5 or more consecutive working days (Article 36(1)(e)).
  • You are already eligible for a pension.
  • You resign unlawfully (without the notice required). This is not covered by clause 9 of Article 34.

3. Job-loss allowance – what the law says

Article 47(1): “The employer shall pay a job-loss allowance to employees who have worked regularly for it for a full 12 months or more and lose their jobs under Article 34(11) of this Code, at one month's wage for each year worked, but no less than 02 months' wages.”

Article 34(11) covers being let go under Article 42 (structural or technological change, economic reasons) or Article 43 (division, merger, consolidation; sale, lease or conversion of the business…).

Before letting people go under Article 42, the employer must consult the grassroots employee representative organisation (where there is one) and give 30 days' notice to the provincial People's Committee and to the employees (Article 42(6)).

The two-month minimum still applies when your counted service is under 24 months – even if almost all your time was covered by unemployment insurance:

Decree 145/2020, Article 8(2): “Where an employee who has worked regularly for the employer for a full 12 months or more loses their job but the working time used to calculate the job-loss allowance under clause 3 of this Article is less than 24 months, the employer shall pay a job-loss allowance of at least 02 months' wages.”

4. How to calculate: what counts and what is deducted

Counted service = total time actually worked for the employer minus time covered by unemployment insurance (UI) and any time already paid out as severance or job-loss allowance (Articles 46(2), 47(2)).

Why deduct UI? Time covered by UI is protected by the UI fund's unemployment benefit – see Social and unemployment insurance.

Time actually worked includes (Decree 145/2020, Article 8(3)(a)): probation; time sent on training; sick and maternity leave paid by social insurance; time off for a work accident or occupational disease while paid by the employer; stoppages that are not your fault; weekly rest, public holidays, annual leave and paid personal leave…

Time covered by UI means time actually covered by UI contributions, plus time when you were not required to be covered but the employer paid you an equivalent amount with your wages (Decree 145/2020, Article 8(3)(b)). Months when the employer did not pay UI for you are not deducted.

Rounding (Decree 145/2020, Article 8(3)(c)): leftover months of 6 or fewer count as half a year; more than 6 count as one year.

The wage used is your average contract wage over the 6 months before you leave (Articles 46(3), 47(3)).

Examples (hypothetical)

  • B worked 4 years and resigned lawfully. The employer paid UI for all 4 years → counted service = 0 → no severance. B files for unemployment benefit within 3 months.
  • C worked 6 years 4 months and resigned lawfully. For the first 2 years the employer did not pay UI; it paid in full for the remaining 4 years 4 months → counted service = 2 years → severance = 2 × ½ = one month's average wage.
  • D worked 5 years with UI paid throughout, then was let go for economic reasons (Article 42) → counted service = 0, under 24 months → job-loss allowance of at least two months' wages.

5. When it must be paid

Article 48(1): “Within 14 working days from the date the employment contract ends, both parties shall settle in full all payments related to each party's interests; this may be extended, but not beyond 30 days, in the following cases”

The cases that allow up to 30 days include the employer ceasing operations, restructuring, technological change, economic reasons, division and merger. At the same time, the employer must pay for annual leave not taken – see Working hours and leave.

6. Common problems in factories

  • Probation, or months when the employer did not pay UI, are left out of severance.
  • Severance is worked out on a lower wage than the 6-month average contract wage.
  • People let go for economic reasons are recorded as “ending by mutual agreement”, so they get only severance (half a month per year) instead of the job-loss allowance (at least two months).
  • No 30 days' notice when letting people go under Article 42.
  • Payment later than 14 working days (or 30 days).

7. What you can do

  1. Ask for the reason your contract is ending and get the decision or notice in writing (Article 45).
  2. Read carefully before signing any “agreement to end the contract”. If the employer is cutting jobs for economic reasons, ask: is this a dismissal under Article 42?
  3. Work it out yourself: total time worked, minus your UI time (check the VssID app), times your 6-month average wage.
  4. Take screenshots of your social insurance and UI record and your last 6 payslips.
  5. If you had UI: file for unemployment benefit within 3 months of the contract ending, at the employment service centre.
  6. If you are not paid in full or on time: ask in writing, then complain – see Keep evidence and complain the right way.
Coming changes

A bill amending the Labour Code is before the National Assembly (November 2026). According to published reports, it does not change the severance or job-loss allowances. Not law yet – this page will check again once it is passed. Decree 145/2020 also has a draft amendment.
