Managing your monthly pay

Checked on 23/11/2026 · Compiled by Peter Tran

In short
  • Your wages are yours. The employer may not force you to spend them on goods or services it chooses, and may not fine you or cut your pay instead of following the disciplinary procedure.
  • Read your payslip every month and compare it with your hours. Deductions are allowed only to compensate for damage to company property, and no more than 30% a month.
  • On payday, split your money according to a plan. Build up an emergency fund so you do not need to borrow when you are ill or lose your job.

Quotations of Vietnamese law below are unofficial translations. The Vietnamese text prevails.

1. What the law says

Your wages are yours to spend

Labour Code, Article 94(2): “The employer shall not restrict or interfere with the employee’s right to decide how to spend their wages; shall not force the employee to spend wages on buying goods or using services of the employer or of another entity designated by the employer.”

The payslip must show all deductions

Article 95(3): “Each time wages are paid, the employer must give the employee a pay statement clearly showing the wages, overtime pay, night-work pay, and the content and amount of any deductions.”

Paid into a bank account: the employer pays the fees

Article 96(2): “Where wages are paid into the employee’s personal bank account, the employer must pay the fees for opening the account and transferring the wages.”

Late pay

Monthly wages are paid once a month or twice a month, on a fixed date (Article 97(2)). Only for reasons of force majeure may the employer pay late, and then no more than 30 days late. If pay is 15 days or more late, the employer must also pay you interest on the late amount (Article 97(4)).

Wage advances: no interest

Article 101(1): “The employee may receive a wage advance on conditions agreed by both parties, and no interest shall be charged.”

When you take annual leave, you may receive an advance of at least your wages for the days off (Article 101(3)).

Deductions: one reason only, at most 30%

Article 102: “1. The employer may deduct from the employee’s wages only to compensate for damage caused by damaging tools, equipment or property of the employer under Article 129 of this Code. 2. The employee has the right to know the reason for the deduction. 3. The monthly deduction shall not exceed 30% of the employee’s monthly wages actually paid after compulsory social insurance, health insurance, unemployment insurance and personal income tax.”

No fines instead of discipline

Article 127(2): it is prohibited to “impose fines or cut wages in place of labour discipline.”

Rotating savings groups (hụi, họ, biêu, phường)

The Civil Code recognises these groups as a customary arrangement. If a group pays interest, the rate must follow the Civil Code (maximum 20% a year). The law prohibits running a group as a form of loan-sharking (Civil Code, Article 471(3) and (4)).

2. Common problems in factories

  • The payslip shows only one “net pay” figure – no overtime, night work or deductions.
  • Money taken for arriving late, or a few hundred thousand dong “fined” for damaged goods – this is a fine in place of discipline, which is prohibited.
  • Deductions for damage without giving the reason, or more than 30% in a month.
  • Pay goes to a bank card but the worker pays the card or transfer fees.
  • Interest charged on wage advances.
  • Workers made to buy meals or goods at a canteen or shop chosen by the company, taken straight out of their pay.
  • Workers never read their payslips and only discover wrong deductions over many months when they leave.

3. Making a monthly spending plan

This section is practical advice, not a legal rule.

Step 1. Know exactly what you take home – the amount that reaches your account after insurance and tax (if any).

Step 2. Split the money on payday, before you spend:

Item Note
Rent, electricity, water Pay first
Food, transport Split by week so you do not run out mid-month
Money sent home Agree a fixed amount with your family
Debt repayments (if any) Pay the highest-interest debt first
Emergency fund Keep separate; do not touch it unless it is urgent
Other Clothes, phone, leisure

Hypothetical example: you take home VND 7,000,000. You might set aside: rent and utilities 1,500,000; food and transport 2,500,000; sent home 1,500,000; emergency fund 700,000; other 800,000. Everyone’s situation is different – adjust it, as long as total spending does not exceed what you take home.

Step 3. Write down what you spend – in a notebook or a phone note, every day. At the end of the month, see where most of the money went.

Step 4. Build an emergency fund. Put a little aside every month, even a small amount. Aim to build up, over time, 1–3 months of spending. This money is for illness, accidents or losing your job – so you do not have to borrow at high interest. Keep it in a bank savings account, separate from your wage account.

Step 5. Overtime and bonuses: put part of them into the emergency fund or towards debts before spending.

With hụi groups: only join with people you know well; record how much you paid and when, with a witness; never put in more than you could afford to lose. When a group collapses, it is usually very hard to get money back.

4. What to do if your payslip is wrong

  1. Photograph your payslip every month and compare it with the hours you recorded yourself.
  2. If you see an unexplained deduction, ask HR by message or in writing for the reason and basis (Article 102(2)).
  3. If you were fined, had more than 30% deducted, were charged interest on an advance or charged card fees, ask the company in writing to pay it back, stating the months and amounts.
  4. If it is not resolved, go to the commune/ward People’s Committee where the company is based or the provincial Department of Home Affairs – see Keep evidence and complain the right way.
  5. How to calculate overtime and night pay: see Wages and overtime.

5. Who to call

  • The company’s HR department.
  • The commune/ward People’s Committee or the provincial Department of Home Affairs.
  • Free legal aid – see Support contacts.
  • If debt is making you stressed or unable to sleep, talk to someone in your family, and read more about looking after your mind at Tâm Hồn Khỏe.
Coming changes

From 01/01/2027, monthly minimum wages rise: Region I VND 5,700,000, Region II VND 5,080,000, Region III VND 4,450,000, Region IV VND 4,040,000 (Decree 399/2026/NĐ-CP, replacing Decree 293/2025/NĐ-CP). Being verified – figures from press reports; will be checked against the full decree. In January 2027, check whether your payslip has been adjusted.
