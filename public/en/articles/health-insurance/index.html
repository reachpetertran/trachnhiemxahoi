Health insurance: using your card at the clinic

Checked on 23/11/2026 · Compiled by Liêm

In short
  • With an employment contract of 1 month or more, your employer must pay health insurance for you – 4.5% of your social insurance wage in total.
  • When you use your registered clinic or are referred correctly, employees usually get 80% of covered costs paid; a visit costing under VND 379,500 is paid 100%.
  • If you work far from home and move, you can use a basic-level facility near your new temporary address at the full rate. You can show your chip ID card, VNeID or VssID instead of a paper card.

Quotations of Vietnamese law below are unofficial translations. The Vietnamese text prevails.

1. Who pays your health insurance

  • With an employment contract of 1 month or more, health insurance is compulsory. The total contribution is 4.5% of your monthly social insurance wage. The employer pays 3% and deducts 1.5% from your wages. Being verified
  • Without an employment contract (self-employed, at home): join as a household. From 01/07/2026 the first member pays 4.5% of the base salary = VND 113,850 a month (VND 1,366,200 a year); the second member onwards pays less.
  • If your payslip deducts 1.5% for health insurance but VssID shows nothing, your employer may not be paying it in. See Social insurance.

2. How much the fund pays

When you go to your registered primary clinic, or are referred correctly, the fund pays at your benefit rate. Employees fall in the “other” group:

Health Insurance Law, Article 22(1)(e): “80% of medical costs for other participants.”

You get 100% when:

  • A single visit costs less than the level set by the Government (Article 22(1)(b)). From 01/07/2026 this is 15% of the base salary = VND 379,500.
  • You use a primary-level facility: commune health stations, family medicine clinics, clinics of district health centres, workplace health units… (Article 22(1)(c)).
  • You have been insured for 5 years in a row or more and your co-payments in the year are more than 6 times the reference level (Article 22(1)(d)). The reference level currently equals the base salary, so the threshold is 6 × VND 2,530,000 = VND 15,180,000 (our calculation).
  • It is an emergency – at any facility (Article 22(5)).

“Covered costs” means drugs and services on the health insurance lists. On-request services and drugs off the list you pay yourself.

3. Working far from home, moving house

Many workers are registered at a clinic in their home province but live and work in another. The law has a specific rule:

Article 22(3): “People registered for primary health insurance care at a specialised-level or basic-level facility who seek care somewhere other than their registered facility because they have changed their temporary residence or place of stay may use a basic-level facility suited to their new temporary residence or place of stay, and the fund pays as set out in clause 1 of this Article.”

The procedure is set by the Minister of Health. The safest option: register your primary clinic near where you live or work – ask HR or the social insurance office.

4. Going somewhere else without a referral

Under Article 22(4), if you go on your own (no referral, no emergency), the fund pays part of your benefit:

Where Fund pays (share of your benefit)
Primary-level facility (commune health station…) 100%
Inpatient at a basic-level facility 100%
Facility that was district level before 01/01/2025 100%
Facility that was provincial level before 01/01/2025 – inpatient 100%
Facility that was provincial level before 01/01/2025 – outpatient 50% (from 01/07/2026)
Inpatient at a specialised-level facility (other cases) 40%

From 01/07/2026, according to the Ministry of Health, outpatient self-referral at some basic-level and specialised-level facilities is paid at 50% of your benefit (many such visits were not paid before). Conditions on the list in Circular 01/2025/TT-BYT are still paid at 100%. From 01/07/2026, people with one of 62 diseases or disease groups under Circular 01/2026/TT-BYT, once diagnosed, can go straight to a specialised-level facility for treatment.

Example (hypothetical): your benefit is 80%, you go on your own for outpatient care at a hospital that used to be provincial level, and covered costs are VND 1,000,000. The fund pays 50% × 80% = 40%, i.e. VND 400,000; you pay VND 600,000.

5. What to bring

According to the Ministry of Health, you can use your chip ID card, the VNeID app or the VssID app instead of a paper health insurance card. Bring a referral letter if you were referred from elsewhere.

6. Common problems

  • Workers far from home stay registered at a clinic in their home village, so care near the factory is paid at a lower rate.
  • The employer deducts 1.5% on the payslip but does not pay it to the social insurance agency.
  • Patients do not know that visits under VND 379,500 are paid 100%, or that in an emergency any facility will do.
  • After leaving a job, people do not check how long their card stays valid.

7. What you can do

  1. Open VssID and check: is your card valid, where is your registered clinic, has your employer paid in full?
  2. Move your registered clinic near where you live or work: ask HR or the social insurance office.
  3. At the clinic: say you are using health insurance and show your chip ID card or VNeID/VssID.
  4. Keep receipts and cost statements after each visit. If what you pay seems unusually high, ask the hospital's health insurance desk or the social insurance office.
  5. When you leave a job: ask how long your card stays valid. With no new contract, join household health insurance so cover does not lapse. If you are receiving unemployment benefit, ask the employment service centre about health insurance during that time.
Coming changes
  • Draft – not law yet. The Ministry of Health is consulting on a draft decree that would raise health insurance contributions step by step from 4.5% to 5.1%, then 5.4% and 6%. Press reports give different start dates for different groups (2027 or 2028). The draft also proposes higher benefits for some groups from 01/04/2027.
  • National Assembly Resolution No. 261/2025/QH15 sets the goal of basic free hospital care within health insurance, step by step, from 01/01/2030.
