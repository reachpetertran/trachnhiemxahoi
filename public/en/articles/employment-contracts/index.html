Employment contracts in Vietnam: 8 things to know before you sign

Checked on 18/11/2026 · Compiled by Liêm

In short
  • A contract of one month or more must be in writing, in 2 copies – you keep one.
  • The employer may not keep your original ID or diplomas, or make you pay a cash or property deposit.
  • If wages are unpaid or late, or you are abused or harassed, you may quit at once without notice.

Quotations of Vietnamese law below are unofficial translations. The Vietnamese text prevails.

1. You must get a written contract – and your own copy

An employment contract must be in writing, in 2 copies: one for you, one for the employer. An electronic contract has the same value as a paper one. Only contracts shorter than 1 month may be oral (with some exceptions set by law).

Labour Code 2019, Article 14(1): “An employment contract shall be concluded in writing and made in 02 copies; the employee keeps 01 copy and the employer keeps 01 copy…”

You must sign your own contract (Article 18). Only for seasonal work or specific jobs under 12 months may a group of workers aged 18+ authorise one member to sign – and then the contract must list each worker's full name, date of birth and signature.

2. A fixed-term contract may be signed at most twice

There are 2 types: indefinite-term and fixed-term (up to 36 months) – Article 20.

  • If the contract ends and you keep working, a new contract must be signed within 30 days. If not, the old contract automatically becomes indefinite-term.
  • A fixed-term contract may be renewed only once. If you keep working after that, the third contract must be indefinite-term (with a few exceptions such as older workers and foreign workers).

3. Probation: once only, with day limits

Only one probation period per job (Article 25):

Type of job Maximum probation
Enterprise manager 180 days
Requires college degree or higher 60 days
Requires vocational secondary level, skilled worker, professional staff 30 days
Other jobs 6 working days

Probation pay must be at least 85% of the pay for that job (Article 26).

4. Three things the employer must NOT do

Article 17. The employer must not: 1. Keep the employee's original identity papers, diplomas or certificates. 2. Require the employee to give a security in money or other property for performing the employment contract. 3. Force the employee to perform the employment contract to pay a debt to the employer.

In plain words: deposits for uniforms, tools or “holding your place”, and keeping your ID card or original diplomas, are all illegal.

5. You may quit at once, without notice, if…

Under Article 35, you may end the contract without notice if:

  • you are not given the job, workplace or working conditions agreed;
  • you are not paid in full or not paid on time;
  • you are mistreated, beaten, insulted, or subjected to forced labour;
  • you are sexually harassed at work;
  • you are pregnant and must stop work on a medical facility's advice;
  • you reach retirement age;
  • the employer gave false information when hiring you that affects the contract.

In other cases you must give notice: at least 45 days (indefinite-term), 30 days (12–36 months), 3 working days (under 12 months).

6. After you leave – full payment within 14 working days

Article 48(1): “Within 14 working days from the termination of the employment contract, both parties are responsible for fully paying all amounts related to each party's rights…” – in some cases this may be extended, but not beyond 30 days.

Annual leave days you have not taken must be paid in money when you leave (Article 113(3)).

7. Common problems found in factories

From audit experience, these contract problems come up most often:

  • No contract, or a signed contract never handed to the worker.
  • The worker never signed, or someone else signed for them.
  • Two months' probation for unskilled workers (beyond the 6-day or 30-day limit).
  • A third fixed-term contract.
  • Deductions for uniform or tool deposits when starting work.
  • Last month's wages or unused leave withheld after resignation.

8. What you can do

  1. Before signing: read the job, workplace, pay and term. Ask if anything is unclear – you do not have to sign on the spot.
  2. After signing: ask for your copy. Photograph every page, keep it on your phone and send a copy to a family member.
  3. Asked for a deposit or original documents: give copies only. If you already handed them over, ask for them back in writing (message or letter) and note the date.
  4. In a dispute: see Keep evidence and complain the right way and the help contacts.
Coming changes

A bill amending the Labour Code was submitted to the National Assembly on 21/10/2026 and is under review. It is not law yet. This article will be updated after the vote.
