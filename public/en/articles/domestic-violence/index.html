Domestic violence: recognising it, safe shelter, no-contact orders, who helps

Checked on 24/11/2026 · Compiled by Liêm

In short
  • Domestic violence is not only beating. Verbal abuse, isolation, controlling money, forced sex, forcing someone to overwork, and throwing someone out of the home are all domestic violence under the law.
  • Victims have the right to protection, a confidential temporary shelter, medical care, counselling, legal aid, and to ask the commune People's Committee chair for a no-contact order.
  • Call 117 – the national domestic violence hotline (free, 24/7). Being beaten or your life is threatened: call 113. A child is being abused: call 111.

Quotations of Vietnamese law below are unofficial translations. The Vietnamese text prevails.

1. What counts as domestic violence

The law lists 17 kinds of behaviour (Article 3(1)). Some common ones:

Law on Domestic Violence Prevention and Control 2022, Article 3(1): “a) Torturing, ill-treating, beating, threatening or other intentional acts harming health or life; b) Insulting, nagging or other intentional acts offending honour and dignity; … e) Preventing a family member from meeting relatives or having lawful, healthy social relationships, or other acts meant to isolate them or put them under constant psychological pressure; … i) Forcing a wife or husband into sexual relations against their will; … o) Forcing family members to study or work beyond their strength, or to contribute money beyond their means; controlling family members' property and income to make them materially, emotionally or otherwise dependent; p) Isolating or confining a family member; q) Unlawfully forcing a family member out of their lawful home.”

Other behaviour on the list: neglecting children, pregnant women, the elderly or people with disabilities; forcing someone to watch violence; spreading private matters to humiliate; forcing child marriage, forcing or blocking divorce; forcing pregnancy or abortion; seizing or destroying property.

Acts between divorced people or people living together as a couple can also be domestic violence (Article 3(2)).

Ill-treating or tormenting a spouse, child or parent can be a crime (Criminal Code, Article 185).

2. Victims' rights

Article 9(1): A victim of domestic violence has the right to “b) ask competent agencies and persons to apply measures to stop violence and to protect and support them under this Law; c) be given a temporary shelter, with the shelter and their private, personal and family information kept confidential…; d) receive health services, counselling, skills to deal with domestic violence, legal aid and social assistance as provided by law; đ) require the person who committed violence to remedy the harm and compensate for damage to health, honour, dignity and property”.

3. Protection measures

No-contact order – the abuser may not come near you:

Who decides How long When
Chair of the commune/ward People's Committee Up to 3 days each time; must decide within 12 hours of a request Violence harms or threatens health or life; you (or your guardian) ask for it or agree (Article 25)
Court handling a civil case between the two of you (e.g. divorce) Up to 4 months On written request, or on the court's own motion to protect life (Article 26)

During a no-contact order the victim chooses where to live (Article 25(5)). Anyone breaking the order can be held in administrative custody (Article 25(6)).

Other measures (Article 22): ordering the violence to stop; the commune police summoning the abuser (mandatory where the victim is a child, a pregnant woman, a woman with a child under 36 months, an older person or a person with a disability – Article 24); temporary shelter arranged by the commune chair (Article 28); medical care (Article 29); legal aid and counselling (Article 30).

Trusted addresses: respected people or organisations in the community who volunteer to shelter victims; the commune People's Committee keeps and publishes the list (Article 36).

Emergency protection: domestic violence victims may stay at a social assistance facility for up to 3 months, or with a host household that receives VND 100,000 per person per day for food (Decree 335/2026/NĐ-CP, Article 16(2), Article 21(2)).

4. Where to report

Article 19(1) – places that receive reports: “a) the People's Committee of the commune where the violence happens; b) the police or border post near where it happens; c) the school where the victim studies; d) the village head, residential group leader or head of the Front work committee in the residential area; đ) the head of a commune-level socio-political organisation; e) the national domestic violence hotline.”

You can report by phone, text message, letter or in person (Article 19(2)). Neighbours, relatives and co-workers can report too.

The national hotline is 117, put into operation by the Ministry of Culture, Sports and Tourism on 8 September 2026: 24/7, free; it takes reports, gives first advice and connects victims to local support services.

5. Common problems

  • Thinking “family matters stay in the family”, or that it only counts if there is a serious injury.
  • A husband takes all his wife's wages, stops her working or seeing her family – this is violence (Article 3(1)(e), (o)).
  • Repeated mediation while the violence continues and the victim still lives with the abuser.
  • Victims not keeping medical records, photos of injuries, or threatening messages.
  • A worker's spouse comes to the factory gate to cause trouble, and the company only sends them away without calling the police.

6. What to do

  1. Safety first. Being beaten or your life is threatened: leave if you can, call 113 or 117.
  2. Prepare an emergency bag: ID card, children's birth certificates, health insurance card, land/house papers (if any), a phone with VNeID, some cash, a charger, any medicines. Send photos of your documents to someone you trust.
  3. Keep evidence: photos of injuries, records from the health station or hospital (they must provide them when you ask – Article 29), messages, recordings, names of witnesses.
  4. Go to the commune/ward People's Committee to ask for a no-contact order and temporary shelter. You can ask verbally; ask the officer to record it and to tell you the result within 12 hours.
  5. If you want a divorce, custody of your children or a share of property: ask the provincial State Legal Aid Centre. Domestic violence victims with financial difficulty qualify for free legal aid (Law on Legal Aid 11/2017, Article 7 – being checked: exact point after amendments; see Help contacts).
  6. If your children are abused or have to witness violence: call 111 for child-protection advice.

7. Who to call

  • Domestic violence (reports, advice, referral): 117 – free, 24/7.
  • In danger: 113. Injured: 115.
  • A child is abused: 111.
  • No-contact orders, shelter: chair of the commune/ward People's Committee; commune/ward police.
  • Free legal aid: the State Legal Aid Centre in each province or city.
Coming changes

No bill to amend the 2022 Law has been found yet. Some articles of Decree 76/2023 refer to the “district level”, which was abolished on 1 July 2025; being checked: the new devolution rules. This article will be updated when anything changes.
