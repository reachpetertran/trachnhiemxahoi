App loans and loan sharks: how to spot and avoid them

Checked on 23/11/2026 · Compiled by Peter Tran

In short
  • For loans between individuals, the agreed interest rate may not exceed 20% a year. Any interest above that has no legal effect.
  • “Black” loan apps often take fees up front, demand access to your contacts and charge interest by the day. Lending at 5 times the maximum rate or more and making illegal profit of VND 30 million or more can be a crime.
  • {'If you are threatened, shamed online, or your relatives are harassed by phone': 'keep the evidence and report to the police. Debt-collection services are a banned business.'}

Quotations of Vietnamese law below are unofficial translations. The Vietnamese text prevails.

1. What the law says

Maximum interest: 20% a year

Civil Code, Article 468(1): “The interest rate for a loan is agreed by the parties. Where the parties agree an interest rate, it shall not exceed 20% a year of the loan amount, unless another relevant law provides otherwise. (…) Where the agreed rate exceeds the limit in this clause, the part of the rate above the limit has no effect.”

“Another law provides otherwise” refers to loans from banks and finance companies, which follow the law on credit institutions. Loans between people – shops, Facebook or Zalo groups and so on – follow the 20%-a-year cap.

Easy to remember: 20% a year ≈ VND 548 per VND 1 million, per day.

When it becomes a crime

Criminal Code, Article 201(1): “A person who, in a civil transaction, lends at an interest rate 5 times the maximum rate set by the Civil Code, making illegal profit from VND 30,000,000 to under VND 100,000,000, or who has been administratively sanctioned for this act or convicted of this crime and not yet had the conviction expunged and offends again, shall be fined from VND 50,000,000 to VND 200,000,000 or sentenced to non-custodial reform for up to 3 years.”

Illegal profit of VND 100 million or more: a fine of VND 200 million to 1 billion or 6 months to 3 years in prison (clause 2).

Five times 20% is 100% a year ≈ VND 2,740 per VND 1 million per day. Apps charging “VND 3,000 per million per day” are at about 109.5% a year.

Collecting debts by threats or public shaming is against the law

  • Threatening force or using psychological intimidation to obtain money may be the crime of extortion – 1 to 5 years in prison (Criminal Code, Article 170(1)).
  • Seriously insulting another person’s dignity or honour may be the crime of humiliating another person. Doing this through the internet or phone networks carries a heavier penalty – up to 2 years in prison (Article 155(1) and (2)(e)).
  • Running a debt-collection service is a banned business (Law on Investment 2025, Article 6(1); the 2020 Law on Investment also banned it from 01/01/2021). Being verified

Your personal data is protected by law

From 01/01/2026, the Law on Personal Data Protection No. 91/2025/QH15 is in force and prohibits buying and selling personal data. You have the right to be informed, to consent, to withdraw consent and to ask for your data to be deleted. Being verified – the provisions have not yet been quoted from the original text.

Savings groups may not be turned into loan-sharking either

Civil Code, Article 471(4): “Organising a savings group as a form of loan-sharking is prohibited.”

2. Signs of a “black” loan app

  • Adverts such as “approved in 5 minutes”, “no collateral”, “bad credit OK” sent by text, Facebook or Zalo.
  • You are told to install the app from a link, not from an official app store.
  • The app demands access to your contacts, photos and messages, or a photo of you holding your ID card.
  • You borrow 3 million but receive only 2 million – the “fees” are taken first.
  • Very short terms (7–14 days), interest by the day, very heavy late penalties.
  • No clear company name, address or licence; no contract, or you cannot download it.
  • You must transfer an “insurance fee” or “disbursement fee” before receiving the money – usually a scam, and the fee is lost.

Hypothetical example: you borrow 3 million through an app, receive 2 million, and must repay 3 million after 7 days. You pay 1 million in “interest and fees” for 2 million over 7 days – 50% a week, roughly 2,600% a year. At the legal cap of 20% a year, the interest on 2 million for 7 days would be only about VND 7,700.

3. Common mistakes

  • Borrowing from one app to repay another → the debt grows very fast.
  • Borrowing at the end of the month because money ran out before payday – when you could ask for a wage advance with no interest (Labour Code, Article 101).
  • Lending your ID card or bank account, or borrowing in your name for someone else.
  • Not keeping the contract, messages or transfer history – so there is no evidence when the threats start.
  • Paying whatever is demanded, including unreasonable “fees”, out of fear of being shamed.

4. What to do

Before borrowing

  1. Ask yourself: is there a way not to borrow? Ask for a wage advance; use your emergency fund – see Managing your monthly pay.
  2. If you must borrow, use a bank or finance company with a name, an address and a clear contract. Ask the bank where you receive your wages.
  3. Read carefully: the amount you actually receive, the total you must repay, the due dates and penalties. Work out the yearly interest yourself.
  4. Do not let an app access your contacts or photos. Do not transfer “fees” up front.

If you have already borrowed from a “black” app

  1. Keep evidence: screenshots of the app and contract, transfer history (amount received, amounts repaid), threatening messages and recordings of calls.
  2. Recalculate: how much you received and how much you have repaid. Interest above 20% a year has no legal effect (Civil Code, Article 468).
  3. Warn your family first that they may receive calls or messages shaming you – so they do not panic or send money to anyone.
  4. If you are threatened, shamed, or people come to your home to cause trouble: call 113 or report to the commune/ward police where you live, with your evidence.
  5. Do not borrow more to repay. List your debts and give priority to banks and finance companies with proper contracts.
  6. For legal advice, see who qualifies for free legal aid in Keep evidence and complain the right way.

5. Who to call

  • Threats or violence: 113, or the commune/ward police where you live.
  • Free legal aid – see Support contacts.
  • If debt pressure leaves you stressed, unable to sleep or feeling trapped, do not carry it alone. Talk to a family member or someone you trust, and see a health service if you need to. Read more at Tâm Hồn Khỏe.
Coming changes

No new legal document changing the 20%-a-year cap or Article 201 of the Criminal Code was found (checked 23/11/2026). Peer-to-peer (P2P) lending is currently allowed only as a test within the scope of a certificate issued by the State Bank of Vietnam (Decree 94/2025/NĐ-CP).
