Informal and day workers: contracts, insurance and domestic workers

Checked on 24/11/2026 · Compiled by Peter Tran

In short
  • If someone gives you work, pays you and supervises you, it can be an employment contract even if it is called “piecework”, “collaboration” or nothing at all. Jobs under one month may be agreed verbally; one month or more needs a written contract and compulsory social insurance.
  • Domestic workers must always have a written contract. The employer may not keep your identity papers or give you work outside the contract.
  • No contract (delivery riders, motorbike taxi drivers, street vendors…): you can join voluntary social insurance, household health insurance and voluntary work-accident insurance (VND 444,000 a year in 2026) yourself.

Quotations of Vietnamese law below are unofficial translations. The Vietnamese text prevails.

1. Is a verbal job an employment contract?

Labour Code 2019, Article 13(1): “Where the parties use a different name but the agreement shows paid work, wages and management, direction and supervision by one party, it is deemed to be an employment contract.”

So the name does not matter. If there are three signs – paid work, and someone managing, directing and supervising you – it is an employment relationship.

Article 14(2): “The parties may conclude a verbal employment contract for a term of less than one month, except in the cases in Article 18(2), Article 145(1)(a) and Article 162(1) of this Code.”

  • Work under one month (a few days as a construction helper, one load of goods): a verbal agreement is allowed.
  • Work of one month or more: there must be a written contract (or an electronic one), with one copy for each side (Article 14(1)).
  • Exceptions – always in writing, however short: workers under 15; domestic workers; and hiring a group of people through a representative (Article 18(2)).

2. When compulsory social insurance applies

Social Insurance Law 2024, Article 2(1)(a): compulsory participants include “people working under an indefinite-term employment contract or a fixed-term employment contract of one month or more, including where the worker and the employer use a different name but the agreement shows paid work, wages and management, direction and supervision by one party”.

Part-time workers whose monthly wage is at least the minimum wage used for compulsory contributions are also covered (Article 2(1)(l)).

So if you work for one employer continuously for one month or more, are given tasks, have your time recorded and are paid wages, you have the right to ask for a written contract and social insurance. See Employment contracts and Social insurance.

3. Domestic workers

Article 161(1): “A domestic worker is a worker who regularly does household work for one or more households.” This includes housework, housekeeping, caring for children, the sick or the elderly, driving, gardening and similar work, but not commercial activities.

Article 162(1): “The employer must conclude a written employment contract with a domestic worker.”

  • The contract must state how and when you are paid, daily working hours and accommodation (Article 162(3)).
  • Either side may end the contract at any time with at least 15 days' notice (Article 162(2)).
  • The employer must pay you the social and health insurance amounts set by law so that you can join yourself (Article 163(2)), and pay your fare home when you leave, unless you end the contract early yourself (Article 163(6)).

Article 165 – the employer is strictly prohibited from: “1. Maltreating, sexually harassing, using forced labour against or using violence against a domestic worker. 2. Assigning a domestic worker work not under the employment contract. 3. Keeping the worker's identity papers.”

Domestic workers have a duty to report an employer who maltreats, sexually harasses or uses forced labour to the authorities (Article 164(4)).

4. No contract: insure yourself

Delivery riders, motorbike taxi drivers, street vendors, self-employed tradespeople and people paid by the job usually work without an employment contract (Labour Code, Article 3(6)). Ride-hailing drivers and delivery riders are currently not in compulsory social insurance; the Ministry of Home Affairs says this group joins voluntary social insurance (reply of June 2026).

Law on Occupational Safety and Health 2015, Article 6(3)(c): people working without an employment contract have the right to “take part in and receive voluntary work-accident insurance as regulated by the Government.”

Type What it covers Cost in 2026
Voluntary social insurance Pension; VND 2 million maternity allowance From VND 330,000 a month, State support 20–50% – see article
Household health insurance Medical care First person VND 113,850 a month – see article
Voluntary work-accident insurance A lump sum if an accident reduces your working capacity by 5% or more 12% of the Region IV minimum wage = VND 444,000 for 12 months; State pays 30% (poor households), 25% (near-poor), 10% (others)

Voluntary work-accident insurance only pays for accidents while doing the occupation and work, at the times and places you registered (Decree 143/2024/NĐ-CP, Article 3). For how the allowance is calculated, with an example, see Farmers and fishers, section 1.

You also have a duty to take responsibility for safety in your own work and to keep people around you safe (Law on Occupational Safety and Health, Article 6(4)).

5. Common mistakes

  • Working for one employer or one site for months on a verbal basis, with no contract and no social insurance.
  • Not knowing the real name or phone number of the person who hired you (the gang boss or contractor) – so when you are not paid, you don't know whom to claim from.
  • Domestic workers whose ID card is kept by the employer, who are made to work in the employer's shop too, and who get no days off.
  • Delivery riders or motorbike taxi drivers injured on a job with no insurance at all, paying every cost themselves.
  • Registering for voluntary accident insurance with the wrong occupation or place of work.

6. What you can do

  1. Keep a work log (on paper or your phone): date, start and finish times, place, who gave you the work, money received. Photograph the workplace; keep messages giving you work and bank transfer records. See Keep evidence.
  2. Write down the full name, phone number and address of the person who hired you and of the site owner.
  3. Working for one employer for one month or more: ask for a written contract and social insurance.
  4. Domestic workers: ask for a written contract before you start; never hand over original documents; agree clearly on hours and days off.
  5. Insure yourself: go to the social insurance office or the commune collection point to register for household health insurance, voluntary work-accident insurance and voluntary social insurance.
  6. Unpaid, beaten, or your papers taken: keep evidence and report to the commune or ward People's Committee or the police.

7. Who to call

  • Threats, assault, being held, papers taken: 113.
  • Accident, serious illness: 115.
  • Tricked, forced to work, trafficked: 111 (free, 24/7).
  • Complaints about pay or contracts; asking for mediation: the commune or ward People's Committee or the provincial Department of Home Affairs.
  • Free legal aid (if you are eligible) and other numbers: Help contacts.
Coming changes
  • A bill amending the Social Insurance Law is before the National Assembly (November 2026). It proposes letting the Government and the National Assembly Standing Committee extend compulsory social insurance to platform workers (ride-hailing drivers, delivery riders, online sellers), but does not yet name specific groups. Not law yet.
  • The draft amended Law on Occupational Safety and Health proposes letting ride-hailing drivers and delivery riders join work-accident insurance. Not law yet.
  • From 01/01/2027, the Region IV minimum wage is expected to be VND 4,040,000 (Decree 399/2026/NĐ-CP, being verified) → voluntary work-accident insurance for 12 months will be VND 484,800 (our calculation).
