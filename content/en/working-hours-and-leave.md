## 1. Normal working hours

> **Labour Code 2019, Article 105(1):** “Normal working time shall not exceed 08 hours per day and 48 hours per week.”

- The employer may set working time **by the week**. Then a day may not exceed **10 hours**, and the week still may not exceed **48 hours** (Article 105(2)).
- The State **encourages** a 40-hour week. This is encouraged, not required.
- **Night work** runs from 22:00 to 06:00 the next morning (Article 106). Night work earns at least 30% extra – see [Wages and overtime](../wages-and-overtime/).
- Any time **beyond** normal hours is overtime. Overtime needs your consent and has limits (Article 107).

## 2. Breaks during a shift and between shifts

> **Article 109(1):** “Employees who work 06 hours or more a day under the working time set out in Article 105 are entitled to a break of at least 30 consecutive minutes during working time; for night work, at least 45 consecutive minutes.”

- On **continuous shifts** of 6 hours or more, the break **counts as working time** (Article 109(1)).
- The 45-minute break applies to shifts of 6 hours or more with **at least 3 hours** between 22:00 and 06:00 (Decree 145/2020, Article 64(1)).
- The employer **may not** put the break at the **start or end of the shift** (Decree 145/2020, Article 64(3)).
- **Changing shifts:** you get at least **12 hours' rest** before moving to another shift (Article 110).

## 3. Weekly rest

> **Article 111(1):** “Every week, employees are entitled to at least 24 consecutive hours of rest. In special cases where weekly rest is impossible because of the work cycle, the employer must ensure an average of at least 04 days off per month.”

- The weekly rest day can be Sunday or another day, but it **must be written in the workplace rules** (Article 111(2)).
- If your weekly rest day **falls on a public holiday**, you get a **day off in lieu on the next working day** (Article 111(3)).
- Working on your weekly rest day is overtime, paid at **at least 200%**.

## 4. Public holidays – full pay

> **Article 112(1):** “Employees are entitled to days off work with full pay on the following public holidays:”

| Holiday | Days |
|---|---|
| New Year's Day (1 January) | 1 |
| Lunar New Year (Tết) | 5 |
| Victory Day (30 April) | 1 |
| International Labour Day (1 May) | 1 |
| National Day (2 September plus 1 adjacent day) | 2 |
| Hùng Kings' Commemoration (10th day of the 3rd lunar month) | 1 |
| **Total under the Labour Code** | **11** |

The exact Tết and National Day dates are decided by the Prime Minister each year (Article 112(3)).

**Vietnam Culture Day – 24 November:** under National Assembly Resolution No. 28/2026/QH16 (effective 01/07/2026), “24 November every year is ‘Vietnam Culture Day’; employees are entitled to a day off work with full pay.” In 2026 it falls on **Tuesday, 24/11/2026**. So from 2026 there are **12 paid public holidays** in total.

- Working on a public holiday: at least **300%**, **on top of** your holiday pay (Article 98).
- Holiday pay is based on **your contract wage** at the time (Decree 145/2020, Article 67(2)).

## 5. Annual leave

> **Article 113(1):** “Employees who have worked for a full 12 months for an employer are entitled to annual leave with full pay under their employment contract, as follows: (a) 12 working days for work in normal conditions; (b) 14 working days for minor employees, employees with disabilities, and people doing heavy, hazardous or dangerous work; (c) 16 working days for people doing especially heavy, hazardous or dangerous work.”

- **Seniority:** every full **5 years** with the same employer adds **1 day** (Article 114).
- **Less than 12 months:** leave is pro-rated to the months worked (Article 113(2)). Calculation: full-year leave ÷ 12 × months actually worked (Decree 145/2020, Article 66(1)).
  - Example: normal work, 7 months worked → 12 ÷ 12 × 7 = **7 days**.
- A month in which working days plus paid days off make up **50% or more** of normal working days **counts as a full month** (Decree 145/2020, Article 66(2)).
- **Time that still counts towards annual leave** (Decree 145/2020, Article 65) includes: probation, if you stay on afterwards; sick leave up to 2 months a year in total; leave for a work accident or occupational disease up to 6 months in total; maternity leave; and work stoppages that are not your fault.
- The **leave schedule** is set by the employer **after consulting** employees, with advance notice. You can agree to take leave in several parts, or **combine up to 3 years** at once (Article 113(4)).
- **Leaving with leave not taken:**

> **Article 113(3):** “Where employees resign or lose their jobs without having taken some or all of their annual leave, the employer shall pay them wages for the days not taken.”

This is based on your contract wage for **the month before** the month you leave (Decree 145/2020, Article 67(3)), and must be paid with your other final payments **within 14 working days** (Article 48).

## 6. Personal leave

Under Article 115, you **tell the employer** and get:

| Situation | Days | Pay |
|---|---|---|
| You get married | 3 | Full pay |
| Your child (birth or adopted) gets married | 1 | Full pay |
| Death of your parent (birth or adoptive), your spouse's parent, your spouse, or your child | 3 | Full pay |
| Death of a grandparent or sibling; a parent or sibling gets married | 1 | Unpaid |

Beyond these, you can agree with the employer on unpaid leave (Article 115(3)).

## 7. Common problems in factories

- In peak season, workers **work several weeks with no day off** – breaking the weekly rest rule and the overtime limits.
- The break is put at the **end of the shift** so people can leave early, or night shifts get only 30 minutes.
- Day–night shift changes **without 12 hours' rest** in between.
- When orders are short, the employer **makes workers use annual leave** for days the factory stops. A stoppage for the employer's reasons is a **work stoppage**, with its own pay rules (Article 99), not your annual leave. The employer sets the leave schedule but must **consult** employees first.
- **No pay for leave not taken** when someone leaves.
- Probation or maternity leave is not counted towards annual leave.

## 8. What you can do

1. **Ask to see the workplace rules** to find your weekly rest day, break times and how leave is scheduled.
2. **Record your hours and days off** every day (start, finish, breaks). Photograph the timesheet if you can see it.
3. **Keep track of your leave balance:** ask HR by message or in writing, and keep the reply.
4. **Before you leave:** count your untaken leave days, put them in your resignation letter and ask to be paid.
5. If you are made to work without days off, or leave is deducted wrongly: ask the employer **in writing**, then complain if it is not fixed – see [Keep evidence and complain the right way](../keep-evidence/).
