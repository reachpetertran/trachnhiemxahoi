## 1. Children in special circumstances

> **Law on Children, Article 10(1):** Children in special circumstances include: “a) children who have lost both parents; b) abandoned children; c) children with no one to rely on; d) children with disabilities; đ) children with HIV/AIDS; e) children who break the law; g) children addicted to drugs; h) children who must leave school to earn a living before completing lower-secondary education; i) children seriously harmed physically or mentally by violence; k) exploited children; l) sexually abused children; m) trafficked children; n) children with life-threatening or long-term illnesses in poor or near-poor households; o) migrant, displaced or refugee children whose parents are unknown or who have no carer.”

These children receive **support** to access social assistance and other help (Article 49(2)(c), (d)); when harmed, they receive **intervention**: medical care, psychological therapy, a safe place to stay, alternative care, legal aid (Article 50).

## 2. Monthly allowance

Under **Decree 335/2026/NĐ-CP, Article 5(1)**, children **under 16** receive a monthly allowance in any of these cases (summary):

- **Abandoned** and not yet adopted.
- **Both parents have died**; or one has died and the other is unknown.
- One parent has died (or is unknown) and the other is **declared missing**, **in prison**, in a compulsory drug rehabilitation centre, a reform school or compulsory education facility, or cared for in a social assistance facility.
- **Both parents** are declared missing, **in prison**, or in such facilities – or each in one of these situations.

From 1 July 2026 the base rate is **VND 540,000 a month** (Article 4(2)). Allowance (Article 6(1)(a)):

| Child's age | Coefficient | Per month |
|---|---|---|
| Under 4 | 2.5 | **VND 1,350,000** |
| 4 and over | 1.5 | **VND 810,000** |

**Turning 16 while still studying** continuously (general education, continuing education, vocational training, a first university degree): the allowance **continues** until the studies end, coefficient 1.5 (Article 5(2), Article 6(1)(b)).

Others who receive allowances: children with HIV/AIDS in poor households; children with severe or very severe disabilities (see [People with disabilities](../disability-rights/)); **single parents in poor or near-poor households raising children under 16** – coefficient 1.0 (VND 540,000) **per child** (Article 5(3), (4), (6); Article 6).

**Carers** (grandparents, aunts, uncles, neighbours…) of a child in the groups above receive a minimum monthly **care support**: coefficient 2.5 for a child under 4, 1.5 for a child aged 4 to under 16 (Article 17(1)).

**How to apply:** the application form, submitted on the **National Public Service Portal**, by post, or at **any One-Stop Service Desk**. The commune/ward chair decides within **7 working days**; the allowance starts from the month of the decision (Article 8).

## 3. School

> **Resolution 217/2025/QH15, Article 2(1):** “Tuition is exempted for preschool children, general-education pupils and learners of the general-education programme in state educational institutions.”

In **private or community-run schools**: tuition **support** at a rate set by the provincial People's Council (Article 2(2)).

Children receiving social allowances also get other **education and training support** under the law (Decree 335/2026, Article 10) – ask the school about learning-cost support, books and boarding meals.

## 4. When a child cannot live safely at home

> **Law on Children, Article 62(2):** children needing alternative care include “children who cannot live with their father or mother for their own safety; children whose parents are unable to protect or raise them, or who are themselves the abusers.”

Four forms of alternative care (Article 61): by **relatives**; by an individual or family **who are not relatives**; **adoption**; in a **social assistance facility**.

Family debt, gambling, a parent's alcoholism or serious illness: a child **has no duty to work to pay off debts**. Making a child work against the law is **child exploitation** – a prohibited act (see [Children at work](../children-at-work/)). Lenders threatening or harassing a child: report to the police (see [App loans and loan sharks](../app-loans-and-loan-sharks/)).

## 5. Common problems

- An orphan lives with grandparents who **do not know** about the allowance, or find the paperwork daunting.
- A 16-year-old's allowance is **cut** although they are still at school – because the family did not report the continued study.
- Parents working far away leave a child with acquaintances, and nobody follows the child's schooling or safety.
- A child leaves school to work and pay the parents' debts.
- Adults see a neighbour's child beaten or left hungry for days but think it is “someone else's family business”.

## 6. What to do

**If you are caring for an orphan, or a child whose parents are in prison or missing:**

1. Fill in the **allowance application** for the child (Form 01) and the **care declaration** (Form 02), and submit them at a One-Stop Desk or on the Public Service Portal (Decree 335/2026, Articles 7, 8). The commune checks the national population database itself.
2. Bring any papers you have (death certificate, missing-person declaration, papers showing a parent is serving a sentence) so officers can check faster.
3. Ask the commune/ward People's Committee about a **health insurance card** for the child and learning support.
4. When the child turns 16 and is still studying: submit a **certificate of enrolment** so the allowance continues.

**If you are a child having a hard time at home:**

1. It is **not your fault** when your parents are in debt, argue or fight.
2. Tell a teacher or an adult you trust. Or call **111** yourself – it is free, and someone answers 24/7.
3. Beaten, thrown out of home, or made to work: call **111** or **113**.

**If you are a neighbour or teacher:** if you see a child beaten, neglected, or leaving school to work – call **111** or tell the commune/ward People's Committee.

## 7. Who to call

- Child protection, advice for children and families: **111** (free, 24/7).
- Domestic violence: **117**. Immediate danger: **113**. Emergency medical: **115**.
- Social allowances, alternative care: commune/ward People's Committee (One-Stop Desk).
- Free legal aid for children: the State Legal Aid Centre in each province or city.
