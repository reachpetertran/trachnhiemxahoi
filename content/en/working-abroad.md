## 1. Who regulates overseas recruitment

Since 01/03/2025, the **Department of Overseas Labour** sits under the **Ministry of Home Affairs** (previously the Ministry of Labour, Invalids and Social Affairs). Only **licensed enterprises** may send workers abroad under contract.

**Before paying anyone**, ask the provincial Department of Home Affairs where you live, or the Department of Overseas Labour, whether the company **holds a licence**. Do not rely on papers shown to you by the broker.

## 2. What fees are lawful

A **service fee** is what a licensed enterprise may collect. Under Law 69/2020/QH14, Article 23:

> **Clause 4(a):** the maximum service fee collected from the worker is “no more than 01 month of the worker's contractual wage for each 12 months of work; for officers and crew on sea-going transport vessels, no more than 1.5 months' wage…”

- It may only be collected **after** the labour supply contract has been approved by the authority **and** you have signed your contract with the enterprise (clause 2(c)).
- If the foreign employer already paid the fee, the enterprise may only collect **the remaining amount** from you (clause 2(d)).
- If you must return early **through no fault of your own**, the enterprise must **refund** the fee for the unworked period, with interest (clause 3).
- Some markets and jobs have **lower caps** (clause 4(c)). Example: a Taiwanese placement agency may charge the worker at most **TWD 1,500 per month** as a service charge (Circular 21/2021/TT-BLĐTBXH, appendix).

<p class="change">Caps on <b>brokerage</b> fees by market (previously in Circular 02/2024/TT-BLĐTBXH, including zero-fee cases such as Japan) are <b>being verified against the replacing regulation</b>. Meanwhile, ask the Department of Home Affairs or the Department of Overseas Labour which cap applies to your job order.</p>

Anyone asking you to pay to “buy” a job order or an EPS place, promising a “guaranteed pass”, collecting money **before** you sign a contract, or charging above the cap – **that is a scam sign.**

## 3. The ILO's 11 indicators of forced labour

The International Labour Organization lists 11 indicators. Seeing **even a few** is a reason to seek help now:

1. Abuse of vulnerability (debt, no language skills, no papers).
2. **Deception** about the job, pay or workplace.
3. **Restriction of movement** – not allowed to leave the dormitory or workplace.
4. **Isolation** – cut off from family.
5. Physical or sexual **violence**.
6. **Intimidation and threats** (threats to call the police, deport you, harm your family).
7. **Retention of passport or identity documents**.
8. **Withholding of wages**.
9. **Debt bondage** – a debt that can never be repaid.
10. **Abusive living and working conditions**.
11. **Excessive overtime**.

Under Vietnam's 2024 anti-trafficking law, victims **and people being identified as victims** receive support (medical care, interpreting, legal aid…).

## 5. What you can do – before you go and once abroad

**Before you go**

1. Check the company's licence (section 1). Read the contract carefully: workplace, job, pay, hours, who pays the air ticket, accommodation.
2. Every payment must have a **receipt in the company's name** – never pay into a personal account.
3. Photograph your passport, visa, contract and receipts – **give a copy to a family member**.
4. Learn the language before you go. You can study English for free at [Tiếng Anh Căn Bản](https://tienganhcanban.com).

**Once abroad**

1. **Your passport and residence card belong to you** – do not hand them to the employer or broker.
2. Record your hours daily and photograph your payslips – see [Keep evidence](../keep-evidence/).
3. Save the numbers in section 4 on your phone.
4. If your life is in danger, call the local police first, then a help line.
