## 1. A 7-item evidence kit

Keep these **from your first day at work**, not only when something goes wrong:

1. **Your employment contract** – photograph every page.
2. **Your payslip** every month. The employer must give a pay statement showing wages, overtime, night pay and deductions (Labour Code, Article 95(3)).
3. **Your own working hours** – write down start, finish and overtime every day. Photograph the time sheet if you can see it.
4. **Your social insurance record** – screenshot the VssID app every month.
5. **Messages and notices** from the company or managers about pay, shifts or discipline.
6. **Medical papers** after an accident or illness.
7. **Names and phone numbers of witnesses** – co-workers who know what happened.

**Store them in 2 places:** on your phone, and a copy with a family member or in cloud storage.

## 2. Ask in writing first

Spoken requests are easy to deny. Ask by **message or letter**, stating: who you are, your department, what happened, which month, the amount you calculate, what you ask for and by when. Keep the request and the reply.

## 3. Where to complain

Since **01/07/2025**, district labour offices no longer exist. Their work moved to:

- the **commune or ward People's Committee** where the company is based (its home affairs / culture-society office);
- the provincial **Department of Home Affairs** – which also appoints a **labour mediator** for each case.

For social insurance problems, also contact the **social insurance office** where your employer pays.

## 4. Steps for an individual labour dispute

Under the Labour Code, individual disputes usually go: **labour mediator → Labour Arbitration Council → court**. Under Article 188(1), these disputes **do not have to** go through mediation first: dismissal or unilateral termination of the contract; compensation or allowances on termination; disputes of domestic workers; social, health, unemployment, and work accident/occupational disease insurance; compensation between workers and companies sending them abroad; disputes of sub-leased workers.

**Time limits** (Article 190, counted from the day you discover the act that violated your rights):

| Request to | Within |
|---|---|
| Labour mediator | 6 months |
| Labour Arbitration Council | 9 months |
| Court | 1 year |

If force majeure or an objective obstacle prevented you from acting in time, that period is not counted (Article 190(4)) – but you must be able to prove it. **Do not wait.**

## 5. Free lawyers: who qualifies?

The **State Legal Aid Centre** (under each provincial Department of Justice) helps for free the people listed in Article 7 of the Law on Legal Aid, including: people with meritorious service, **members of poor households**, **children**, ethnic minorities living in especially disadvantaged areas…; and people **in financial hardship** who are older people, **people with disabilities**, **victims of domestic violence**, **victims of human trafficking**, people living with HIV, relatives of martyrs, people affected by Agent Orange…

Workers in ordinary labour disputes are **not automatically** eligible unless they belong to one of these groups. Ask the centre directly whether you qualify.

## 6. Protect yourself when you complain

- Hand over **copies** only; keep the originals.
- Note the date, time and who you spoke to.
- If threatened or assaulted: call **113** (in Vietnam).
- If you feel stressed or cannot sleep for a long time, talk to someone you trust, and look after your mental health – see [Tâm Hồn Khỏe](https://tamhonkhoe.com) (in Vietnamese).
