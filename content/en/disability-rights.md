## 1. Who is a person with a disability

> **Law on Persons with Disabilities 2010, Article 2(1):** “A person with a disability is a person who has an impairment of one or more body parts or a reduction in function, shown as a disability, that makes work, daily life or study difficult.”

**Six types** (Article 3(1)): mobility; hearing and speech; vision; **neurological and mental**; intellectual; other.

**Three levels** (Article 3(2)):

- **Very severe**: cannot carry out daily personal care alone.
- **Severe**: cannot carry out some daily personal care alone.
- **Mild**: all other cases.

People left with a disability after a **work accident or occupational disease** can also apply to have their level assessed.

## 2. Getting a disability certificate

> **Article 18(1):** “A person with a disability, or their lawful representative, who needs the level of disability assessed sends an application to the People's Committee of the commune where they live.”

- Within **30 days**, the commune chair convenes the **Disability Assessment Council** and tells you when it will meet (Article 18(2)).
- The Council observes you directly, asks you to do simple daily tasks and uses a questionnaire (Article 17).
- Within **5 working days** of the Council's conclusion, the commune posts it publicly and **issues the disability certificate** (Article 18(4)).
- If you disagree, ask for assessment by the **Medical Assessment Council** (Article 15(2)).

## 3. Monthly allowance

From **1 July 2026** the social assistance base rate is **VND 540,000 a month** (Decree 335/2026/NĐ-CP, Article 4(2), Article 32(3)). Allowance = base rate × coefficient (Article 6(1)(e)):

| Who | Coefficient | Per month |
|---|---|---|
| Person with a **very severe** disability | 2.0 | VND 1,080,000 |
| **Child** or **older person** with a very severe disability | 2.5 | VND 1,350,000 |
| Person with a **severe** disability | 1.5 | VND 810,000 |
| **Child** or **older person** with a severe disability | 2.0 | VND 1,080,000 |

Plus a monthly **care allowance** (Article 17(2)):

- A person with a severe or very severe disability who is **pregnant or raising one child under 36 months**: coefficient 1.5 (VND 810,000); pregnant and raising a young child, or raising two or more children under 36 months: 2.0.
- A **household directly caring for** a person with a very severe disability: coefficient 1.0 (VND 540,000) per person.

People receiving a monthly social allowance have their **health insurance paid by the State** (Law on Health Insurance, Article 12 – State-budget-funded group; **being checked**: exact point after the 2024 amendments).

Provinces may set higher rates (Article 4(3)). **How to apply**: the application form (Form 01 of the Decree), submitted online on the **National Public Service Portal**, by post, or at **any One-Stop Service Desk**; the commune chair decides within **7 working days** (Articles 7, 8). The allowance starts **from the month** of the decision – apply early so you do not lose months.

## 4. Training and work

> **Law on Persons with Disabilities, Article 33(2):** “Agencies, organisations, enterprises and individuals may not refuse to recruit a person with a disability who meets the recruitment criteria, or set recruitment criteria contrary to law so as to limit the job opportunities of persons with disabilities.”

> **Labour Code, Article 160 – prohibited:** “1. Making a worker with a mild disability and 51% or more loss of working capacity, or a severe or very severe disability, work overtime or at night, unless the worker agrees. 2. Making a worker with a disability do heavy, hazardous or dangerous work on the list issued by the Minister of Labour, Invalids and Social Affairs without their consent after the employer has given them full information about the work.”

Employers must also (Labour Code, Article 159):

- Provide working conditions, **tools**, safety and **periodic health checks suited** to workers with disabilities.
- **Consult** workers with disabilities on decisions affecting their rights and interests.

**Training**: **free vocational counselling** and the right to choose a trade according to ability (Law on Persons with Disabilities, Article 32(1)). **Free job counselling** at employment service centres (Article 33(1), (5)). Self-employed: **preferential-rate loans** (Article 33(6)).

Businesses where **30% or more of workers have disabilities** get corporate income tax exemption, preferential loans and priority for land rental (Article 34).

## 5. Common problems

- Job ads saying “good health, no disability” for work a person with a disability can do.
- Putting workers with disabilities on night shifts or overtime without asking them.
- Not adapting the workplace (access ways, seating, visual notices for deaf workers).
- Families with a severely disabled member who have **never applied for a certificate**, so receive no allowance.
- Workers who lost working capacity in a work accident not knowing they can apply for a disability assessment.

## 6. What to do

1. **No disability certificate yet**: write an application to the commune/ward People's Committee where you live. Bring any medical papers (discharge papers, assessment results).
2. **Already have one**: fill in the allowance application and submit it on the Public Service Portal or at a One-Stop Desk. Ask about health insurance and the care allowance.
3. **At work**: if you do not want night shifts or overtime, tell your manager clearly (in writing if possible) – the law protects your right to say no.
4. **Refused a job or treated badly because of disability**: note the date, who said what, and keep messages. Report to the Department of Home Affairs (labour inspection) or the commune People's Committee. See also [Discrimination and harassment](../discrimination-and-harassment/).
5. **Need a free lawyer**: people with disabilities who have financial difficulty qualify for legal aid; this group widens from 1 January 2027 (Law 05/2026/QH16). See [Help contacts](../../contacts/).

## 7. Who to call

- Disability certificate, allowance: commune/ward People's Committee (One-Stop Desk).
- Training, jobs: the provincial employment service centre.
- Workplace violations: the provincial Department of Home Affairs.
- A child with a disability is abused or neglected: **111**.
- Emergencies: **113**, **115**.
