## 1. What counts as trafficking

> **Law on Prevention and Combat of Human Trafficking 2024, Article 2(1):** “Human trafficking is the recruitment, transport, harbouring, transfer or receipt of a person for the purpose of receiving money, property or other material benefits, sexual exploitation, forced labour, removal of body parts or other inhumane purposes, by the use of force, threat of force, deception or other tricks.”

For **anyone under 18**, recruiting, moving, harbouring, handing over or receiving them for these purposes **is trafficking** – even without force, threats or deception (Article 2(1), second paragraph).

> **Article 2(3):** “Forced labour is the use of force, threat of force or other tricks to compel a victim to work against their will.”

**“Other tricks”** include abusing **marriage brokerage, job brokerage, study-abroad advice, adoption, or overseas tourism** to deceive; taking advantage of someone's **debt, desperation or dependence**; and giving sleeping pills, alcohol or drugs to make a person lose awareness (Article 2(6)).

**“Other inhumane purposes”** include forced begging, **forced marriage, forced childbearing** and being forced to commit crimes (Article 2(5)).

These are **crimes**: the Criminal Code covers human trafficking (Article 150), trafficking of persons under 16 (Article 151) and forced labour (Article 297).

## 2. Warning signs

**Before you leave:**

- An “easy job with high pay”, no skills or language needed, **leave right away**, someone pays your ticket.
- Someone you met online promises a job, marriage, or a trip abroad.
- No contract, or one without the company name, address, job and pay.
- You are told to **cross the border by back roads**, or enter on a tourist visa and “stay on to work”.
- The broker asks to keep your ID card or passport.

**At the workplace:**

- Your **documents or phone are taken**, you cannot go out, you are locked in or guarded.
- You must **repay a “debt”** (ticket, food, lodging, a “purchase price”) that keeps growing and never ends.
- Very long hours with no pay or withheld pay; beatings; threats against your family.
- You are forced to run **online scams**, sell sex, or marry someone.

One or two signs do not always mean trafficking. But **documents taken + a debt that never ends + not allowed to leave** is a very serious sign – get help.

## 3. Victims' rights

> **Article 3(9) and (10) – prohibited:** “Insulting, stigmatising or discriminating against victims or persons being verified as victims.” – “Disclosing information about victims or persons being verified as victims without their consent or that of their lawful representative, except as otherwise provided by law.”

Under **Article 6**, victims and people being verified have the right to: ask for protection for themselves and their relatives; be informed of their rights; receive support **or refuse it**; have their private life, home and workplace **kept confidential**; receive a document confirming victim status; and receive **compensation**.

> **Article 4(5):** “Depending on the specific case and on the relevant law, a victim who commits a violation as a direct consequence of being trafficked may be exempt from administrative penalties or criminal liability for that act.”

So people forced to do wrong (for example forced to run scams, or taken across a border illegally) **may not be punished** – do not let fear of a fine stop you from reporting.

## 4. Support available

Under **Articles 37–45**, victims who are Vietnamese citizens receive:

| Support | Main content |
|---|---|
| Basic needs | Temporary shelter, food, clothing, personal items (Art. 38) |
| Medical | Health check, first aid, emergency care; **health insurance paid for the first year** if they have none (Art. 39) |
| Interpreting | If they do not understand Vietnamese (Art. 40) |
| Legal | Help to replace documents and ID; legal aid in the criminal case (Art. 41) |
| Travel | Bus or train fare home and meals on the way (Art. 42) |
| Psychological | Support for up to 3 months (Art. 43) |
| Schooling | Under-18s: tuition, textbooks and school supplies for the first year and the next (Art. 44(1)) |
| Training, jobs | Counselling, vocational training, help to find work (Art. 44(2)) |
| Allowance, loans | One-off initial hardship allowance; may be considered for preferential loans from the Vietnam Bank for Social Policies (Art. 45) |

People **still being verified** in Vietnam get support from basic needs through psychological support (Article 37(3)). Trafficking and forced-labour victims who need emergency protection may stay at a **social assistance facility** for up to 3 months (Decree 335/2026/NĐ-CP, Article 21(2)).

## 5. Common problems

- A broker “keeps your passport and ID safe for you”.
- Workers sign a debt note for tickets and fees, then must work it off with no end date.
- A family takes a “deposit” or “bride price” and lets a daughter marry abroad through an unlicensed broker.
- Returning victims are gossiped about or told they “brought it on themselves” – this is **prohibited** (Article 3(9)).
- People who return do not report because they fear a fine for crossing the border illegally.

## 6. What to do

**Before accepting a job or travelling:**

1. Get the company name, address, job, pay and hours **in writing**. For work abroad, check the company is licensed (see [Working abroad](../working-abroad/)).
2. **Keep your own** ID and passport. Photograph them and send copies to family.
3. Give family the address you are going to and the recruiter's phone number; agree a daily check-in time and **a code word** that means danger.

**If you are being held or forced:**

1. If it is safe, call **111** (in Vietnam) or **113**. Message family your location, the workplace name, vehicle number plates.
2. Abroad: contact the **Vietnamese embassy or consulate** or the local police. Vietnamese missions must coordinate rescue, verification and return (Article 29).
3. Family in Vietnam can **report for you**: call 111, or go to the police or commune/ward People's Committee (Article 22).

**After you get out or come home:**

1. Report to the nearest **commune/ward People's Committee or police**; they must receive you and provide support (Article 27).
2. Ask for the **document confirming victim status** to access the support in section 4.
3. Ask the provincial **State Legal Aid Centre** for free help when dealing with the police and courts.

## 7. Who to call

- Suspected trafficking or forced labour, in Vietnam or abroad: **111** (free, 24/7).
- In danger or held against your will: **113**.
- Injured or seriously ill: **115**.
- Abroad: the Vietnamese embassy or consulate; the local police. Some support numbers for workers in Japan and Taiwan: see [Help contacts](../../contacts/).
- Free legal aid: the State Legal Aid Centre in each province or city.
