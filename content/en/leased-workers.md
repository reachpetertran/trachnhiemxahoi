## 1. What labour leasing means

> **Labour Code 2019, Article 52(1):** “Labour leasing means an employee concludes an employment contract with an employer that is a labour leasing enterprise, and is then transferred to work under the direction of another employer while maintaining the employment relationship with the employer that concluded the contract.”

In plain words: **you sign with company A** but **work at company B**. A pays your wages and insurance; B directs your daily work.

## 2. Limits set by law

- Leasing lasts **at most 12 months** (Article 53(1)).
- Company B may use leased workers only to: meet a sudden temporary increase in work; replace workers on maternity leave, injured, ill from occupational disease or doing civic duties; or obtain highly qualified technical staff (Article 53(2)).
- Company B **may not** use leased workers to: replace workers who are on strike or in a labour dispute; replace workers dismissed for restructuring, technology change, economic reasons or mergers; or without a specific agreement on who compensates work accidents (Article 53(3)).
- Company B **may not** transfer you to another employer, and **may not** use workers supplied by an **unlicensed** company (Article 53(4)).
- Company A must lodge a **VND 2 billion deposit** and hold a **labour leasing licence** (Article 54; Decree 145/2020, Article 21).

## 3. Only certain jobs may be leased

Decree 145/2020 (Appendix II) allows 20 jobs, including: interpreting, translation, shorthand; secretary, administrative assistant; reception; tour guiding; sales support; project support; programming production machinery systems; manufacturing and installing TV and telecom equipment; operating, inspecting and repairing construction machinery and production electrical systems; cleaning buildings and factories; document editing; bodyguard, security guard; telemarketing and phone customer care; financial and tax matters; car repair and testing; scanning, industrial technical drawing, interior decoration; driving; work on sea-going ships; on oil and gas rigs; on aircraft. <span class="tag chk">Being verified against the original appendix</span>

**Production line work** (sewing, assembly, packing…) is **not** on this list.

## 4. Equal pay and equal treatment

> **Article 58(3):** leased workers must be “paid no less than the employees of the host company with the same qualifications doing the same work or work of equal value”.

- Company B **may not discriminate** in working conditions between you and its own staff (Article 57(2)).
- Night work and overtime must be **agreed with you** (Article 57(3)) – it must be voluntary, as for any worker.
- Company A must **tell you** the content of the leasing contract between A and B (Article 56(2)). That contract may not give you fewer rights than your own employment contract (Article 55(3)).
- You may agree with A to end your contract in order to **be hired directly by B** (Article 58(5)).

## 5. Common problems in factories

- “Outsourced” workers used for production jobs **not on the list**.
- Leased workers paid lower wages, allowances or bonuses **than direct employees** doing the same work.
- Continuous leasing beyond 12 months.
- Workers supplied by a company **without a leasing licence**.
- No one pays social insurance for leased workers, or no one is clearly responsible for work accidents.

## 6. What you can do

1. **Ask clearly** which company you are signing with, and whether it holds a labour leasing licence. Get your copy of the contract.
2. **Compare your pay** with direct employees doing the same job. Keep your payslips – see [Keep evidence](../keep-evidence/).
3. **Check your social insurance** monthly on VssID – see [Social insurance](../social-insurance/).
4. If you are treated differently, or company B breaks the agreement: **complain to company A** (Article 58(4)), then to the provincial Department of Home Affairs if not resolved. Disputes between leased workers and the host company **do not have to** go through mediation first (Article 188).
