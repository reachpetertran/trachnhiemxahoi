## 1. What the employer must do straight away

> **Law on Occupational Safety and Health 2015, Article 38(1):** the employer must “promptly give first aid and emergency care to an employee who suffers a work accident and advance the costs of first aid, emergency care and treatment for an employee who suffers a work accident or occupational disease”.

Article 38 also requires the employer to:

- **Pay medical costs** from emergency care until your condition is stable: the co-payment and items not covered by health insurance (if you have it); **all** costs (if you do not) – clause 2.
- **Pay your full wages** while you are off for treatment and rehabilitation – clause 3.
- **Refer you for a medical assessment** of your loss of working capacity – clause 6.
- **Prepare your file** to claim from the work accident insurance fund – clause 9.
- **Give you suitable work** for your health when you return – clause 8.

The wage used for these payments includes **base wage, wage allowances and other supplements** (clause 10), not just the base wage.

## 2. Compensation from the employer

Article 38(4) – if the accident was **not entirely your own fault**, or you have an occupational disease:

| Loss of working capacity | Employer pays at least |
|---|---|
| 5% – 10% | 1.5 months' wages |
| 11% – 80% | 1.5 months + 0.4 month for each extra 1% |
| 81% or more, or death | 30 months' wages |

If the accident was **your own fault**, the employer must still pay an **allowance** of at least **40%** of the amounts above (clause 5).

Payment is due within **5 days** of the assessment result, or of the publication of the investigation report for a fatal accident (clause 7).

## 3. Accidents outside the factory and on the way to work

- Working outside on the employer's instructions and injured by someone else, or by an unknown person: the employer **must still compensate** as in section 2 (Article 39(1)).
- Injured **on the way from home to work or back**, on a reasonable route and at a reasonable time, by someone else or an unknown person: the employer pays the **40% allowance** (Article 39(2)).
- The employer **did not pay** work accident insurance for you (when you are compulsorily covered): besides compensation, the employer must also pay an **amount equal to** what the insurance fund would have paid (Article 39(4)).

**No employer benefits** if the accident was caused by: a personal conflict unrelated to work; deliberate self-injury; illegal drug use (Article 40).

## 4. Benefits from the work accident insurance fund

If you pay compulsory social insurance, the fund pays extra benefits when your loss of working capacity is **5% or more** (Articles 45, 46). An accident counts when it happens:

- at work during working hours – **including** breaks, meals, using the toilet, washing, breastfeeding;
- outside the workplace or hours, while doing work requested by the employer;
- on the way to or from work, on a reasonable route and at a reasonable time.

Benefits (Articles 48, 49):

- **5% – 30%** loss: a **lump sum**, starting at 5 times the statutory base wage, plus an amount based on years of contributions.
- **31% or more**: a **monthly allowance**, starting at 30% of the statutory base wage, plus an amount based on years of contributions.

Occupational diseases must be on the list of 35 diseases in Circular 60/2025/TT-BYT – see [Occupational safety and health](../safety-and-health/).

## 5. Common problems in factories

- No log of work accidents, even minor ones.
- Serious accidents not reported to the labour authority.
- Treatment days deducted from annual leave, or left unpaid.
- Workers never sent for assessment, so they do not know what they are owed.
- Pressure on workers to sign a statement that the accident was their own fault.

## 6. What you can do

1. **Report immediately** to your supervisor or manager (Article 34). If serious, call **115** (in Vietnam).
2. **Write down** the time, place, machine or task, and witnesses. Take photos if safe.
3. **Do not sign** papers you have not read or that are untrue.
4. **Keep** all medical papers, receipts and discharge papers.
5. Once your condition is stable, **ask for an assessment** of your loss of working capacity.
6. Calculate what you are owed using the table in section 2 and compare. If anything is missing, ask in writing – see [Keep evidence and complain](../keep-evidence/). Disputes about compensation and work accident insurance **do not have to** go through mediation first (Labour Code, Article 188).
