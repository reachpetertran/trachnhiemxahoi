## 1. Two ages to remember

> **Law on Children, Article 1:** “A child is a person under 16 years of age.”

> **Labour Code 2019, Article 143(1):** “A minor worker is a worker under 18 years of age.”

The minimum working age is **15**; under 15, only certain work allowed by law is permitted (Article 3(1)).

> **Law on Children, Article 26:** “Children have the right to be protected in every form from labour exploitation; not to work before the legal age, for longer than allowed, or in heavy, hazardous or dangerous work as regulated by law; and not to be given work or workplaces that harm their personality and all-round development.”

**Child exploitation** includes “forcing children to work in breach of the labour law” (Law on Children, Article 4(7)) – and it is **strictly prohibited** (Article 6(3)).

## 2. What each age may do

| Age | Allowed | Maximum hours |
|---|---|---|
| **Under 13** | Only arts, physical education and sport; must not harm development; needs approval from the labour authority of the provincial People's Committee (Article 145(3)) | 4 hours a day, 20 hours a week |
| **13 to under 15** | Only **light work** on the official list (below) | 4 hours a day, 20 hours a week; **no** overtime, **no** night work |
| **15 to under 18** | Most work, except banned work and workplaces (section 3) | 8 hours a day, 40 hours a week; overtime and night work only in some listed occupations |

Hours are set by **Article 146** of the Labour Code.

**The 12 groups of light work** for 13- and 14-year-olds (Circular 09/2020/TT-BLĐTBXH, Appendix II), in short:

1. Performing arts.
2. Sport.
3. Software programming.
4. Some traditional crafts (making conical hats, weaving mats, making rice noodles, rice crackers, brocade…) – **except any step using toxic chemicals**.
5. Some handicrafts (embroidery, dough figurines, folk paintings, paper boxes…).
6. Weaving household items from rattan, bamboo, sedge, water hyacinth…
7. Wrapping spring rolls, sweets and cakes – **not operating packing machines**.
8. Raising silkworms.
9. Weeding clean vegetable gardens; harvesting clean vegetables and fruit in season.
10. Herding livestock on farms.
11. Helping untangle and mend fishing nets; drying seafood.
12. Trimming threads, sewing buttons and buttonholes, boxing handmade textiles.

## 3. Work and workplaces BANNED for under-18s

> **Labour Code, Article 147(1) – banned work:** “a) Carrying, lifting or moving loads beyond the physical capacity of a minor; b) Producing or trading in alcohol, beer, tobacco, psychoactive or other addictive substances; c) Producing, using or transporting chemicals, gas or explosives; d) Servicing and maintaining equipment and machinery; đ) Demolishing buildings; e) Melting, blowing, casting, rolling, stamping or welding metal; g) Diving at sea, offshore fishing”.

> **Article 147(2) – banned workplaces:** “a) Underwater, underground, in caves or tunnels; b) Construction sites; c) Slaughterhouses; d) Casinos, bars, dance clubs, karaoke rooms, hotels, guesthouses, saunas, massage parlours; lottery outlets, video-game services”.

Circular 09/2020 adds more, for example: operating boilers, ice-making or freezing systems; fitting dies on forging, stamping, pressing or metal-cutting machines (Appendix III); places with noise, dust, chemicals or vibration above permitted limits; contact with disease-causing micro-organisms; working on scaffolds or hanging ropes higher than 2 m; in pits deeper than 5 m; kneeling, lying or bending in cramped spaces for more than 4 hours a day (Appendix IV).

## 4. What an employer of minors must do

- Get **consent from a parent or guardian**, and keep a **separate register** (name, date of birth, current job, health check results) (Article 144(3)).
- Give the young person the chance **to study** and train (Article 144(4)).
- For workers **under 15** (Article 145(1)): sign a **written contract** with both the child and their legal representative (parent…); hours **must not clash with school**; have a **health certificate** and repeat health checks at least **every 6 months**; provide age-appropriate safety.

## 5. What about chores and helping the family?

> **Law on Children, Article 37(2):** children have the duty “to study, train, keep family customs, and help their parents and family members with tasks suited to their age, sex and development.”

Sweeping, cooking, looking after younger siblings and light help to parents are **normal and useful**. What to avoid: work that is **too heavy**, **dangerous** (spraying pesticides, going offshore on fishing boats, carrying heavy loads), or so much work that a child **drops out of school or has no time to rest and play**.

## 6. Common mistakes

- Restaurants, karaoke bars and guesthouses hiring 16- or 17-year-olds to serve or clean rooms – **banned workplaces**.
- Workshops putting 14-year-olds on evening shifts or overtime – **under-15s may not work at night or overtime**.
- Under-18s working as construction helpers, welding, spraying pesticides or going offshore fishing.
- No parental consent, no register, no health check.
- Children leaving school to earn money for the family.
- “Easy work, high pay” recruiters taking children far from home and keeping their phones and papers.

## 7. What you can do

**If you are the young person working:**

1. You have the right to **say no** to heavy or dangerous work, and to night work if you are under 15.
2. **Do not hand** your birth certificate, ID card or phone to strangers or employers to keep.
3. If you are hit, shouted at, kept from leaving or forced to work: tell your **parents, teacher** or an adult you trust straight away. Call **111** – free, 24/7.

**If you are a parent or relative:**

1. Compare your child's work with the table in section 2 and the banned list in section 3.
2. Ask the employer: has the family given consent, what are the hours, does it clash with school? A child under 15 must have a written contract.
3. Put **school first**. If the family is struggling, ask the commune People's Committee about fee support, scholarships and support for poor and near-poor households.
4. Be wary of offers of “high-paid work” far from home for children. If a child is being exploited: call **111**.

## 8. Who to call

- A child is exploited, abused or made to work; someone is tricked or trafficked: **111** (free, 24/7).
- Immediate danger, being held, being beaten: **113**.
- Accident, serious illness: **115**.
- Reporting an employer who hires children unlawfully: the commune or ward People's Committee or the provincial Department of Home Affairs.
