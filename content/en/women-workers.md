## 1. What the law says

**During pregnancy and while raising a young child**

> **Labour Code 2019, Article 137(1):** “The employer may not make an employee work at night, work overtime or travel far on business in the following cases:
> a) Pregnant from the 7th month, or from the 6th month if working in highland, remote, border or island areas;
> b) Raising a child under 12 months old, unless the employee agrees.”

> **Article 137(2):** a woman doing heavy, hazardous or dangerous work, or work harmful to reproduction and child-raising, who is pregnant “and informs the employer shall be moved to lighter, safer work or have her working day reduced by 1 hour without any cut in wages, rights or benefits until her child is 12 months old.”

> **Article 137(3):** “The employer may not dismiss an employee or unilaterally terminate her employment contract on the grounds of marriage, pregnancy, maternity leave or raising a child under 12 months…” If the contract expires during this time, she “shall be given priority to sign a new employment contract”.

The employer also **may not discipline** a pregnant worker, a worker on maternity leave or a worker raising a child under 12 months (Article 122(4)(d)).

**Daily breaks**

> **Article 137(4):** “A female worker is entitled to a 30-minute break each day during menstruation and a 60-minute break each day during working time while raising a child under 12 months. She still receives full wages under the employment contract for these breaks.”

Decree 145/2020/NĐ-CP, Article 80, adds:

- Menstruation breaks: **at least 3 working days a month**; you tell the employer when (clause 3(a)).
- The 60-minute break is “to breastfeed, express and store milk, and rest” (clause 4(a)).
- If you choose to keep working (with the employer's agreement), you are **paid extra** for that time (clauses 3(c) and 4(c)).
- Employers with **1,000 or more women workers** **must** provide a room for expressing and storing breast milk (clause 5).
- Periodic health checks for women include a **gynaecological examination** (clause 1).

**Maternity leave**

> **Article 139(1) (amended by the 2025 Population Law, from 01/07/2026):** “A female worker is entitled to 6 months of maternity leave before and after childbirth; for a second child, 7 months; leave before the birth may not exceed 2 months.” <span class="tag chk">Being verified</span>

For twins or more, the mother gets 1 extra month for each child from the second. After maternity leave you **return to your old job**; if it no longer exists, the employer must give you another job paying no less (Article 140).

## 2. Money from social insurance

If you paid compulsory social insurance for **at least 6 of the 12 months** before the birth, you receive maternity benefits (Social Insurance Law 2024, Article 50(2)).

- **Antenatal check-ups:** up to 5 times, no more than 2 days each (Article 51).
- **Monthly maternity benefit:** **100%** of your average insured wage over the last 6 months (Article 59(1)).
- **Lump sum at birth:** 2 times the reference level per child (Article 58(4)). The reference level currently equals the base salary of VND 2,530,000, so about **VND 5,060,000 per child** (from 01/07/2026).
- A husband paying social insurance gets leave when his wife gives birth: 5 working days; 7 if she has a caesarean or the baby is born before 32 weeks (Article 53(2)). The 2025 Population Law adds **10 working days** for a second child. <span class="tag chk">Being verified</span>
- If you stop work before the birth but already meet the contribution condition, you **are still paid** (Article 50(4)).

## 3. Work to avoid

Circular 10/2020/TT-BLĐTBXH lists jobs harmful to reproduction and child-raising:

- **55 jobs** for all women workers – for example pouring molten metal, welding inside closed tanks, carrying more than 50 kg, running non-automated dyeing or stentering machines.
- **38 more groups of jobs** during **pregnancy or while raising a child under 12 months** – for example direct contact with lead, benzene, mercury, formaldehyde, organic solvents or pesticides; mixing dyes; carrying more than 20 kg; working where the air is 40°C or more (summer); cramped, bent-over postures.

The law does **not ban** you from these jobs, but the employer must **publish** them, give you full information on the risks so you can choose, provide health checks and ensure safety (Labour Code Article 142; Circular 10/2020, Article 11). When you are pregnant, tell the employer so you can move jobs or work 1 hour less on the same pay (Article 137(2)).

With a certificate from a medical facility that continuing to work will harm the baby, you may **quit without notice** or **suspend your contract** (Article 138).

## 4. Common problems in factories

- Women from the 7th month of pregnancy put on night shifts or overtime lists.
- No 30- or 60-minute breaks, or breaks “paid for” with output targets or bonuses.
- Contracts not renewed because the worker is pregnant.
- Asking “do you plan to have children?” or requiring pregnancy tests at recruitment.
- After maternity leave, moving the worker to a lower-paid job.
- Not publishing the list of jobs in the workshop that are harmful to reproduction.

## 5. What you can do

1. **Tell the employer in writing** when you know you are pregnant (keep a copy), with your antenatal record if you have one.
2. If your work is heavy, hazardous or involves chemicals: **ask to be moved or to work 1 hour less**, citing Article 137(2).
3. Note the dates you were put on nights or overtime, or denied the 30/60-minute break.
4. Check your social insurance record on the VssID app – see [Social insurance](../social-insurance/).
5. If you are let go or your contract is not renewed because of pregnancy: **do not sign** a “voluntary resignation”; keep all papers; complain – see [Keep evidence and complain](../keep-evidence/).

Fines (Decree 283/2026/NĐ-CP, Article 26(2)): **VND 10–20 million** for each breach such as night work or overtime from the 7th month of pregnancy, denying the 30- or 60-minute break, or dismissal because of pregnancy. This is the amount for an individual; a company (organisation) pays **twice** as much (Article 7). The employer must also pay wages for the breaks you were denied and take you back to work (clause 3).

## 6. Who to call

- Abdominal pain, bleeding or fainting at work: call **115**.
- Employer breaking the law: the provincial Department of Home Affairs where you work; free legal aid – see [Support contacts](../../contacts/).
