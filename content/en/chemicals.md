## 1. What the law says

> **Law on Chemicals 2025, Article 23(1):** “Organisations and individuals that produce or import chemicals must classify and label them before putting them into use or on the market…”

Classification follows the **GHS** – the United Nations' common system (clause 2). Hazardous chemicals are labelled under the law on goods labels **and** GHS guidance (clause 4).

> **Article 25(2):** “Organisations selling hazardous chemicals must provide the safety data sheet to the buyer. Organisations and individuals buying hazardous chemicals must ask the seller for the safety data sheet and keep it at the place where the chemical is stored.”

> **Article 33(3):** “Workers involved in chemical activities must be trained in chemical safety and provided with personal protective equipment suited to their assigned tasks.”

> **Article 36(1):** “Organisations and individuals carrying out chemical activities must train workers in chemical safety periodically.”

Places storing hazardous chemicals must have warning information, **chemical safety rules** and a signal system. When a chemical has several hazards, the warning symbols must show **all** of them (Article 14(1)(b)).

You also have the right to be informed of hazards and to refuse dangerous work – see [Safety and health at work](../safety-and-health/).

## 2. Reading a label: three things first

**Pictogram** – a red-bordered diamond with a black symbol on white. The GHS has 9:

| Symbol | Meaning |
|---|---|
| Flame | Flammable |
| Flame over circle | Oxidiser – makes fires burn harder |
| Exploding bomb | Explosive |
| Gas cylinder | Gas under pressure – may explode if heated |
| Liquid eating into a hand and metal | Corrosive – burns skin, damages eyes |
| Skull and crossbones | Acutely toxic – can kill |
| Exclamation mark | Irritates skin and eyes; harmful if inhaled or swallowed |
| Person with a star on the chest | Long-term health hazard: cancer, lung damage, harm to fertility |
| Dead fish and tree | Harmful to aquatic life |

**Signal word** – “**Danger**” (more severe) or “**Warning**” (less severe).

**Hazard statements** and **precautionary statements** – for example “causes severe skin burns”, “wear gloves and eye protection”.

Signs in stores and workshops must show the chemical identifier, pictogram, signal word and hazard statement (under Decree 26/2026/NĐ-CP). <span class="tag chk">Being verified</span>

## 3. Reading the safety data sheet (SDS, MSDS)

Under the GHS, an SDS has **16 sections**. If you handle the chemical, read at least:

- **Section 2 – Hazard identification:** how it can harm you.
- **Section 4 – First aid:** what to do after contact with skin or eyes, inhalation or swallowing.
- **Sections 5 and 6 – Fire fighting, spills.**
- **Section 7 – Handling and storage:** what it must not be stored with.
- **Section 8 – Exposure controls and personal protection:** which gloves, mask and goggles to use.

Imported hazardous chemicals must be declared with an SDS **in Vietnamese** (Decree 26/2026/NĐ-CP, Article 6). <span class="tag chk">Being verified</span> If the SDS is only in a foreign language, ask the employer for a Vietnamese version.

**Pregnant women and mothers of babies under 12 months:** many chemicals (benzene, lead, mercury, formaldehyde, organic solvents, pesticides…) are on the list of jobs harmful to reproduction and child-raising (Circular 10/2020/TT-BLĐTBXH). See [Women workers](../women-workers/).

## 4. Common problems in factories

- Chemicals decanted into small bottles or cans **without labels** – sometimes into drinking-water bottles.
- SDSs that are **old, incomplete**, only in English, or kept in the office instead of at the storage point.
- Cotton gloves and surgical masks issued for work that needs chemical-resistant gloves or a respirator.
- Acids stored next to bases, oxidisers next to solvents.
- Eating, drinking or smoking in mixing or painting areas.
- No eyewash or emergency shower near corrosive chemicals.
- No training, or workers only asked to sign the training register.

## 5. What you can do

1. **Before use:** read the label. No label – **do not use it**; tell your team leader.
2. **Ask to see the SDS** for the chemical you use. Read sections 2, 4 and 8.
3. **Wear the protective equipment** listed in section 8. If it is damaged or does not fit, ask for a replacement.
4. **Never** pour chemicals into drinking bottles. **Never** mix chemicals on your own.
5. **Contact with a chemical:** follow section 4 of the SDS. Usually: rinse with plenty of clean water, remove contaminated clothing, then see a doctor. Take the label or SDS with you.
6. **Spill, leak or strange smell:** leave the area, report it at once, and do not clean up without protective equipment.
7. Write down the date, time, chemical name and symptoms. If illness continues, ask for an **occupational disease check-up** – see [Work accidents and occupational diseases](../work-accidents/).

## 6. Who to call

- Poisoning, chemical burns, difficulty breathing: call **115**.
- Fire, explosion or a large chemical leak: call **114**.
- No training or protective equipment: raise it with the employer in writing; then you can report to the Department of Home Affairs where you work – see [Keep evidence and complain](../keep-evidence/) and [Support contacts](../../contacts/).
